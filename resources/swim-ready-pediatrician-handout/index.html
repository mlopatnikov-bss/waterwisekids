Is your child ready for swim lessons?

The American Academy of Pediatrics' 2026 drowning-prevention policy recommends beginning swim classes after a child's first birthday. Readiness and family circumstances still matter, and lessons are only one layer of protection.

For babies under 1: the AAP says there is no evidence that infant swim lessons reduce drowning incidence. Parent-and-child water familiarization may be enjoyable, but it must not be presented as drowning protection.

Discuss readiness

  • Physical and emotional development
  • Comfort around water and new adults
  • Health or developmental considerations
  • Family exposure to pools, beaches or boats
  • Whether the class matches the child's age and demonstrated skills

Keep every layer

  • Close, attentive adult supervision
  • Four-sided pool fencing and secure barriers
  • Properly fitted life jackets where appropriate
  • Caregiver CPR and emergency preparation
  • Lessons that never promise a child is “drown-proof”

Questions for the clinician

  • Is there any health or developmental reason to adapt or delay a class?
  • What should we tell the school about medications, sensory needs or prior water experiences?
  • Which water exposures in our family's routine deserve extra planning?

Questions for the swim school

  • How is a new swimmer assessed, and how do you respond to distress?
  • Who issued the instructors' credentials, and are they current?
  • What is the maximum group size at this level?
  • Which specific skills are taught and used for advancement?
