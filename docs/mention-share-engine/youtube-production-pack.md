## 1. When can swim lessons start?

**Hook:** “The old one-size-fits-all ‘wait until age four’ rule is not current AAP
guidance.”

**Script:** The American Academy of Pediatrics says families may consider swim
lessons after a child's first birthday, based on readiness, health, exposure to
water and family circumstances. That does not mean every one-year-old is ready,
and infant lessons have not been shown to prevent drowning. Lessons are one layer:
close supervision, four-sided pool barriers, appropriate life jackets and adult
CPR readiness still matter. Ask whether the program matches your child’s
development and whether the instructor explains its limits honestly.

**On-screen limitation:** “After age 1 may be considered; readiness varies.
Lessons never make a child drown-proof.”

**Source/landing page:** AAP 2026 Prevention of Drowning policy; link
`/education/when-to-start-swim-lessons.html` and `/medical-review-policy/`.

## 2. Why WWK will not publish a national average yet

**Hook:** “Seventeen verified prices are useful examples—not a national
benchmark.”

**Script:** Swim-lesson prices are hard to compare because tuition can mean one
lesson, a monthly plan, a seasonal package or a membership with separate fees.
WaterWiseKids normalizes official provider observations by lesson and duration,
but the current public pilot has only 17 observations. Our precommitted national
gate requires at least 200 unique locations, 40 states, coverage in every Census
region and provider type, source freshness, concentration limits and a second
review. Until those gates pass, publishing a national average would create false
precision. The honest result today is the dataset and the missing-data list.

**On-screen limitation:** “Pilot examples only. No national, state or metro
average is publishable.”

**Source/landing page:** `/education/swim-lessons-cost.html` and
`/assets/data/price-index/pilot-observations.json`.

## 3. Switching swim schools without guessing a level

**Hook:** “A level name is not a portable credential.”

**Script:** Different swim schools package skills under different names, so a
child’s old level rarely maps one-to-one to a new program. The WaterWiseKids
crosswalk uses broad skill bands—water comfort, floating and recovery,
independent movement, stroke development and advanced skills—to help a parent
ask better questions. It is not a placement decision. Bring the prior progress
report, describe what the swimmer can do without help, and let the receiving
school assess the child in its own environment.

**On-screen limitation:** “Approximate skill bands only. The receiving school
must assess the swimmer.”

**Source/landing page:** `/tools/swim-level-crosswalk/`.

## 4. Why law-by-state pages often overclaim

**Hook:** “A model code is not automatically the law where you live.”

**Script:** Pool barriers, child life-jacket rules and lifeguard requirements can
come from statutes, regulations, adopted building codes and local ordinances.
Copying a model code or another website’s summary can miss scope, exceptions and
local enforcement. WaterWiseKids currently publishes only a three-state pilot
for Florida, New Jersey and Pennsylvania. Every module links to government text,
states a review date and warns about local rules. The other jurisdictions stay
unpublished until the same verification is complete.

**On-screen limitation:** “Plain-English research, not legal advice. Check the
controlling state and local authority.”

**Source/landing page:** `/education/swimming-pool-fence-laws-by-state.html`.

## 5. Compare the complete price, not the headline

**Hook:** “Two ‘$30 lessons’ can have very different annual costs.”

**Script:** Before comparing tuition, write down seven fields: lesson count,
lesson length, class format, maximum ratio, required registration or membership
fees, commitment or cancellation rules, and whether the price is a starting rate
or a published rate for the exact class. Keep subsidized municipal programs and
commercial programs labeled separately. If a provider leaves a field unstated,
record ‘not stated’ instead of guessing. That is how WWK’s price methodology
avoids turning unlike products into a misleading average.

**On-screen limitation:** “A normalized price is a comparison aid, not a quality
rating or recommendation.”

**Source/landing page:** `/education/swim-lessons-cost.html`.

## 6. What a transparent swim school should disclose

**Hook:** “A badge should tell you what was disclosed—not certify a school as
safe.”

**Script:** Ask a swim school to state the instructor credential issuer, maximum
class ratio by level, background-check policy, water temperature, pricing and
required fees, make-up rules, and adaptive-lesson availability. WaterWiseKids’
Transparency Standard records those disclosures and their source dates. It is
not an inspection, accreditation, safety certification or endorsement. Payment
never changes the default directory order or makes a disclosure complete.

**On-screen limitation:** “Disclosure badge only—not inspection, certification,
endorsement or a paid ranking signal.”

**Source/landing page:** `/transparency-standard/` and
`/ownership-disclosure/`.
