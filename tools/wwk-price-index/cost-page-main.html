Pilot dataset methodology

What counts as one observation

One row represents one product configuration at one location on one date. Base and weekend schedules, member and community rates, and group, semi-private, and private lessons remain separate. A package is normalized only when its lesson count is published. We record mandatory fees separately instead of hiding them in a per-lesson number.

Sources we accept

We accept current official provider pages, official price sheets, and government recreation documents. We exclude directory estimates, search snippets, third-party blogs, social comments, and prices inferred from unrelated locations. This official-source rule also excludes providers that do not publish prices, which may bias the sample. “Starting at” remains labeled. If a source does not state duration or ratio, the field remains blank rather than being guessed.

How this pilot sample was chosen

The ten locations are a purposive convenience sample selected to test provider-type, schedule, fee, subsidy, package, and duration fields. They were not randomly selected. None is in New Jersey or Pennsylvania, none is owned by the WaterWiseKids owner, and no British Swim School price is included. Chain locations can share pricing, so future statistics will count unique locations and report provider strata separately rather than treating every row as independent.

Products we will not blend

  • Group, semi-private, private, and parent-child lessons.
  • Member and nonmember prices.
  • No-commitment and multi-month commitment prices.
  • Subsidized public programs and ordinary commercial prices.
  • 30-minute lessons and longer lessons unless duration is known and the comparison is explicitly duration-adjusted.

When WaterWiseKids may publish a future index result

A national result requires at least 200 unique locations across at least 40 states, at least 25 locations in every U.S. Census region, and a separate minimum for the product being described: 150 group, 75 private, 50 semi-private, or 50 parent-child locations. It also requires at least 25 locations in each provider group—chains, independents, municipal programs, and nonprofits/YMCAs—no single chain may exceed 15% of the location sample, every source must be official, and at least 90% of sources must have been checked within 90 days.

A state result requires at least 15 locations and three provider types, with no provider above 35%. A metro result requires at least 12 locations and three provider types, with the same concentration limit. Clearing a gate does not make a convenience sample statistically representative; published releases will describe the sampling limits and show subgroup results.

A fair way to compare two programs

  1. Normalize the tuition: package price ÷ published lesson count.
  2. Match the format: compare group with group and private with private.
  3. Check instructional time: record the scheduled minutes rather than assuming every class lasts 30 minutes.
  4. Include the first-month extras: registration, annual, membership, and required equipment fees can change the initial total.
  5. Read the commitment: a lower price tied to eight or twelve months is not the same offer as a flexible monthly price.
  6. Consider access: scholarships and subsidized programs should be clearly labeled, not treated as evidence that every family can buy at that price.

Data quality and corrections

Every published row includes the source URL, verification date, normalization rule, and qualifiers. “Verified on” means we checked the published source on that date; it does not mean the provider updated the page then. The automated checker recomputes each normalized price, rejects duplicate IDs and unofficial source types, checks segment and regional coverage, and prevents a national headline while any publication gate is unmet. Arithmetic validation does not replace a second source read, so release evidence also records a separate verification pass.

This pilot's current-price window ends February 9, 2027. After that date the page displays a stale warning and the release guard fails until every retained row is rechecked or withdrawn.

Frequently asked questions

What is the national average cost of swim lessons?

WaterWiseKids is not publishing one yet. This pilot does not meet the precommitted national coverage rules, so turning it into a national average would imply precision the data does not support.

Why not average the prices already on this page?

The sample is small and uneven, multiple products can come from one location, and a subsidized public class is not interchangeable with a commercial group class. A simple average would be easy to calculate and easy to misuse.

How should I compare monthly tuition?

Divide tuition by the number of included lessons, keep required fees separate, and note whether the billing cycle contains four lessons or varies with the calendar. Then compare only products with similar class formats, durations, and commitments.

Are the listed programs recommended by WaterWiseKids?

No. Inclusion means only that an official, public price could be verified under the pilot rules. It is not a safety certification, quality score, or endorsement.
