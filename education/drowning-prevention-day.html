Drowning Prevention Day: What It Is, When It Is, and What Families Can Do

World Drowning Prevention Day is observed every year on July 25. Here is why the United Nations created it, what the global numbers say, and the practical steps that protect children at home, at the pool and in open water.

Quick Answer: Drowning Prevention Day, formally World Drowning Prevention Day, falls on July 25 every year. The United Nations General Assembly adopted a resolution on global drowning prevention in April 2021 that established the day, and the World Health Organization (WHO) leads its awareness work. WHO estimates that about 300,000 people drowned worldwide in 2021, and about 9 in 10 of those deaths happened in low- and middle-income countries. For families, the day is a reminder to use layers of protection: a barrier around water, a named adult watching, swim lessons, life jackets and CPR training.

What is World Drowning Prevention Day?

World Drowning Prevention Day is an annual United Nations observance, held on July 25, that draws attention to the number of people who drown each year and to the low-cost measures that prevent it. It is also called International Drowning Prevention Day or simply Drowning Prevention Day. Governments, lifesaving organizations, health agencies, schools and swim programs use the day to share safety messages, publish local data and push for the policies that keep people away from dangerous water.

The day exists because drowning is one of the most preventable causes of death, yet it receives far less public attention than many other injuries. A child can slip below the surface in seconds and without a sound. That makes prevention, which means acting before anyone is in trouble, far more effective than rescue alone. Our guide to what silent drowning looks like explains why even attentive adults miss it.

When is Drowning Prevention Day?

Drowning Prevention Day is observed every year on July 25. The 2026 observance was Saturday, July 25, and the next one falls on July 25, 2027. The date does not move, so you can plan a family, classroom or club activity well ahead. Many programs run a full themed week around the date. Each year WHO and its partners promote a campaign theme, so check the WHO website for the current year's materials before you plan.

The date lands in the middle of the northern-hemisphere summer, when pools, lakes and beaches are busiest. That timing is useful, but the risk does not end in August. Home pools, bathtubs, buckets, ponds and indoor water-park or vacation pools all carry risk outside summer, which is why we treat drowning prevention as a year-round habit rather than a once-a-year event.

Why was a day for drowning prevention created?

The United Nations General Assembly adopted a resolution on global drowning prevention in April 2021, declaring July 25 as World Drowning Prevention Day. The resolution called on countries to act on drowning as a public health problem and asked WHO to coordinate the effort. It followed years of advocacy from lifesaving groups that pointed out how many deaths were going uncounted and how little national planning existed.

Before that, drowning was often treated as an unavoidable accident. The resolution reframed it as a preventable health issue, in the same way that road safety and vaccination became subjects of national plans. In 2023 the World Health Assembly adopted its own resolution on accelerating drowning prevention, and WHO published its first global status report on drowning prevention in December 2024. In the United States, the National Water Safety Action Plan serves a similar coordinating role.

What do the drowning numbers say?

WHO estimates about 300,000 people died from drowning worldwide in 2021, which is more than 30 people every hour. About 9 in 10 of those deaths occurred in low- and middle-income countries. Nearly half of all drowning deaths are among people under 29, and about a quarter are among children under 5. Children without adult supervision are at particularly high risk.

The United States has a much lower rate than the global average, but drowning remains a leading cause of death for young children. According to the CDC, drowning is the leading cause of death for children ages 1 to 4, and the agency has reported more than 4,500 fatal unintentional drownings a year in recent data. Our drowning statistics and facts page and the child drowning statistics dashboard break the numbers down by age and setting.

Two points from the data matter most for parents. First, most young children who drown do so at home, in moments when no one expected them to be near water. Second, drowning is quiet. It rarely involves splashing or calling for help, so the signs described in our guide to the signs of drowning are worth learning before you need them.

What does WHO recommend to prevent drowning?

WHO recommends a small set of community-level measures with strong evidence behind them. They are listed below, with what each looks like in an ordinary family or neighborhood.

  • Install barriers that control access to water. At home this means a four-sided fence with a self-closing, self-latching gate. Our pool fence requirements guide covers the dimensions.
  • Provide safe places away from water for young children. In communities without fences, WHO supports supervised day-care programs for preschoolers. At home, the same idea is a secure play space while adults are busy.
  • Teach school-age children basic swimming, water safety and safe rescue skills. Find a program through the swim school directory or start with our beginner swim lessons guide.
  • Train bystanders in safe rescue and resuscitation. Learn CPR and keep our drowning CPR quick card handy.
  • Set and enforce safe boating, shipping and ferry rules. For families this includes Coast Guard-approved life jackets; see our life jacket guide.
  • Improve flood risk management. This is mainly a community and government measure, but families in flood-prone areas should know evacuation routes and avoid floodwater.

No single measure is enough on its own, which is the idea behind the five layers of protection: when one layer fails, another is still in place.

What can families do on Drowning Prevention Day?

The most useful thing a family can do on July 25, or on any day this week, is turn the day's message into one concrete change at home. You do not need an event. Pick from this list and finish it in a single sitting.

  1. Check every barrier. Walk the fence and gate, and test that the gate latches by itself. Our pool safety fence checklist takes about 15 minutes.
  2. Name a Water Watcher. Before anyone enters the water, decide out loud which adult is watching, with no phone in hand. Use the Water Watcher card and read about touch supervision for toddlers.
  3. Empty what does not need to hold water. Buckets, wading pools and bathtubs are a real risk for toddlers; see bucket and small-container drowning.
  4. Book or review swim lessons. The American Academy of Pediatrics supports lessons for many children starting around age 1. Ask a prospective school how it measures progress; our guide to choosing a swim school lists what to check.
  5. Check life jackets. Make sure each one is Coast Guard-approved, fits the child's weight and is not worn out.
  6. Learn what to do in an emergency. Call 911, get the child out of the water, and start CPR if they are unresponsive and not breathing normally. Our drowning emergency guide gives the steps, and a certified class gives you practice.

How can schools and swim programs take part?

Classrooms, camps and swim schools can use Drowning Prevention Day as a hook for a themed week of short, repeatable activities. A five-minute talk on the pool rules, a game that practices the reach-or-throw rescue, and a take-home checklist for parents are enough. Our water safety games for kids include activities that fit a themed week.

Swim school owners can also use the day to start a conversation with local families about lesson options, scholarships and safety checks. If you run a program, you can add or update your listing in our directory so families searching for lessons can find you.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is World Drowning Prevention Day?

World Drowning Prevention Day is observed every year on July 25. The 2026 observance was on Saturday, July 25, and the next is on July 25, 2027.

Who created Drowning Prevention Day?

The United Nations General Assembly adopted a resolution on global drowning prevention in April 2021 that established July 25 as World Drowning Prevention Day. The World Health Organization coordinates the awareness work with partner organizations.

How many people drown each year?

WHO estimates about 300,000 people drowned worldwide in 2021, and about 9 in 10 of those deaths occurred in low- and middle-income countries. In the United States, the CDC has reported more than 4,500 fatal unintentional drownings a year in recent data.

What is the best way to prevent child drowning?

No single measure is enough. The most reliable approach is layers of protection: a four-sided fence with a self-latching gate, a named adult watching without distractions, swim lessons, Coast Guard-approved life jackets in open water, and CPR training for caregivers.

What can I do on Drowning Prevention Day?

Make one concrete change at home: check the pool fence and gate, name a Water Watcher before every swim, empty buckets and wading pools, review life jackets, book swim lessons, and learn CPR.
