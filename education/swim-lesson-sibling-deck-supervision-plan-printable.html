Swim Lesson Sibling and Pool Deck Supervision Plan

Bringing a baby, a toddler or an older child to a sibling’s swim lesson? Name who watches whom, seat each child where they can be seen and say every hand-off out loud. This page puts the plan in one place so the pool deck stays calm and safe.

📅 Step 1: The Basics

Swimmer’s name and class (level, day, time)
School and instructor
Siblings who come along (names and ages)
Adults who can come (names)
Where siblings may sit (ask the school)
Phone number the school can reach
Do this first. Ask the school where siblings may sit and whether children are allowed on the wet deck. Write the answer here so everyone follows the same rule.

🧭 Step 2: Who Watches Whom

  • One named adult for each child who is not in the water. Write the name next to each child below.
  • A named adult for any child who cannot swim well. That adult stays within arm’s reach.
  • Say the hand-off out loud. “You have the baby.” Wait for a yes before you step away.
  • Phones away near the water. Keep eyes on the child, not the screen.
  • If I am the only adult, I sit where I can see the lesson and the other children. Ask for a second adult if I cannot.
  • The instructor teaches the class, not my other children. Never count on staff to watch a sibling.

Remember. A dry seat away from the pool edge with a clear view of the class is usually best for a young child. Keep strollers and bags away from the edge.

🚫 Step 4: Our Deck Rules

  • Walk, no running. Wet decks are slippery.
  • Stay in our seats. Ask before moving.
  • Never go near the water without a grown-up. Not even to look.
  • No pushing, no splashing the class. Respect the other swimmers.
  • Older siblings get a job, not a toddler. They should not be in charge of a young child near water.
  • Quiet voices during class. It helps the swimmer hear the instructor.

🎒 Step 5: Bag Check

  • Towels and dry clothes for everyone. Wet, cold children get upset faster.
  • Safe snacks for each age. No hard foods for babies and toddlers.
  • A quiet activity for each sibling. A book, a puzzle or a drawing pad.
  • Water bottles. For the swimmer and for siblings.
  • Swim bag items for the swimmer. Cap, goggles and swimsuit, as the school asks.
  • Diapers, wipes and swim diapers if needed. Check the school’s diaper rules.

🚿 Step 6: Arrival, Changing and Leaving

Who takes the swimmer to change
Who holds the youngest child
Who carries the bags
Who does the headcount before leaving
Meeting spot if we get separated
  • Young children stay within reach in the locker room. It is busy, wet and crowded.
  • Headcount at the pool door and again at the car. Say the names out loud.

💙 Step 7: If Someone Gets Upset

Where my swimmer will see me (and the wave signal)
If the baby cries, who steps out and who stays
If a sibling needs the bathroom, who goes and who watches the rest
If the class needs a parent in the water, who has the baby
A plan is one layer, not the only one. Close, undistracted supervision, a designated water watcher, barriers around pools and a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket near open water all matter. Never leave a young child alone on a pool deck, even for a moment. Lessons do not make a child drown-proof, at any age.

✅ Step 8: Our Family Agreement

We agree to this plan (names)
Adult who will review it with the children
Date we will review it again
Changes after the first week
A line to remember: “Name it, seat it, say it out loud.” Name the adult for each child, seat children where they can be seen and say every hand-off out loud.

Spectator, sibling and deck rules differ by school, so follow each program’s written policy. Guidance: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (Drowning Prevention); American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org).
