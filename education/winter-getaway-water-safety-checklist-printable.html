Winter Getaway Water Safety Checklist

For warm-weather trips to resorts, rentals, condos, beaches and relatives’ homes. Refresh skills before you go, sweep the property in the first hour, name a Water Watcher every swim, and swim only near a lifeguard at the beach.

📅 Two to Four Weeks Before

  • Skill check. Can my child get back to the surface, float on their back, turn to the wall and climb out? If not since summer, book refresher lessons.
  • Ask the host. Pool, hot tub, pond, canal or lake on or next to the property? Four-sided fence with self-closing, self-latching gate? Door locks or alarms?
  • Ask about lifeguards. Is the resort pool or nearby beach guarded? What hours this season?
  • Talk to relatives. If visiting family, agree who watches the children and when.

🧳 Pack

  • Each child’s own life jacket. U.S. Coast Guard-approved and fit-checked. No floaties or water wings.
  • Bright swimsuits that are easy to spot in the water.
  • Water Watcher card or tag to hand from adult to adult.
  • Printed address of where you’re staying, for 911.

🔑 First Hour After Arrival

  • One adult holds the kids while another walks the property.
  • Find all water: pools, hot tubs, fountains, ponds, canals, buckets and coolers.
  • Test every pool gate: closes and latches by itself.
  • Lock doors and windows to the pool area; lock the hot tub cover.
  • Tell the host about anything broken, and set a plan to cover it.

🏊 Every Swim

  • Name a Water Watcher. No phone, no book. Hand off out loud and rotate every 15–20 minutes.
  • Arm’s reach for young children and weaker swimmers. If you get out, the child comes with you.
  • Pool rule: no going near the pool without a grown-up. Feet first in water you don’t know.

🌊 Beach Days

  • Swim only near a lifeguard. Check hours and flags every day.
  • Rip current plan: relax, don’t swim straight to shore, swim along the shoreline, then face the shore and call or wave for help.
  • Helping someone: get a lifeguard, call 911, throw something that floats. Don’t swim out.

🚑 Our Emergency Box (fill in when you arrive)

Address where we’re staying
Nearest emergency room
Lifeguard hours: pool / beach
Today’s beach flag
Water Watchers today
Host or front desk phone
If a child is missing, check the water first. If a child is in the water: get them out, shout for help, call 911 and follow the dispatcher’s instructions.
Remember: swim lessons are one layer of protection and do not make children drown-proof. Keep close, constant adult supervision, barriers around pools, life jackets near open water and a named Water Watcher whenever children are in or near water.
