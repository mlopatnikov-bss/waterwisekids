Swim Lesson Bathroom and Potty Break Checklist

Restroom needs are part of lesson day. Restroom stop before class, a potty signal, an adult escort, a calm accident plan. Share this page with everyone who brings your child.

🚻 Step 2: Before Class

  • Make a restroom stop right before class. Try the toilet or change the diaper in the restroom, not at the pool edge.
  • Pack the right swim diaper if my child is not toilet trained. Ask the school what it requires, such as a snug swim diaper with a close-fitting cover.
  • Rinse in the shower if the school asks. Ask about local practice at the first visit.
  • Tell the instructor if my child is learning to use the toilet. Ask how the instructor wants to handle a request.
  • Practice the potty signal at home. A short phrase like “I need the potty” is easy to remember in a new place.

🙋 Step 3: During Class

  • Go right away when my child signals. Do not ask a child to wait until it is urgent.
  • Walk my child to the restroom myself. Young children should not go alone; hold a hand and use walking feet.
  • Plan who watches the other children. Siblings should not be left alone on the deck.
  • Keep my eyes on the water and my child. Restroom trips are supervision hand-offs; make each one clear.
  • Never leave a young child alone in a locker room. Wet floors and open doors are a slip and wandering risk.

🧼 Step 4: If an Accident Happens

  • Stay calm and do not shame my child. Accidents are normal for young swimmers.
  • Tell the instructor right away. Bring my child out of the water.
  • Follow the school’s procedure. The pool may close briefly for cleaning.
  • Keep a child with diarrhea out of the pool. Ask my pediatrician and the school when it is safe to return.
  • Wash hands with soap and water. Both my child and I, before leaving the restroom.

👕 Step 5: After Class

  • Take my child to the restroom again. Try the toilet or change the diaper.
  • Rinse off and change into dry clothes. A warm child is calmer and easier to dress.
  • Pack a change of clothes, wipes and a plastic bag. Add a spare swim diaper if needed.
  • Check the swim bag before leaving. Pack wet items separately.
  • Never leave a child alone in the car. Not even briefly.

Young children are never alone near water or in the restroom. If a child is in trouble in the water, get help and call 911 or my local emergency number. Lessons do not make a child drown-proof, at any age.

This checklist is a family planner, not medical advice. Follow your pediatrician’s guidance and ask the school about its swim diaper, restroom and illness policies. Guidance: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (Healthy Swimming); American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org).
