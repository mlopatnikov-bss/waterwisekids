Swim Lessons with Ear Tubes Checklist

Ear tubes are not always a reason to skip lessons, but your ENT’s advice comes first. Use this page to get answers, pack the right gear, brief the instructor and know when to sit a class out. Your child’s ENT sets the rules; this page is a planner.

🩺 Step 1: Ask Your ENT

  • Are plugs needed? Pool, beach, lake: ask about each and write the answer below.
  • Any limits on diving or deep submersion? Ask about putting the head fully underwater.
  • When can lessons start after surgery? Get a date or a note in writing.
  • Which symptoms mean call right away? Drainage, pain and fever are common examples; ask for your child’s list.
  • Ask about untreated water Lakes, rivers and the ocean carry more germs than a chlorinated pool.

🧢 Step 2: Gear Check Before Each Lesson

  • Plugs, cap or headband fit snugly. Test at home in the bath first.
  • Spare plugs are in the swim bag. Pack a clean towel for the outer ear.
  • Goggles fit and are separate from ear protection. Goggles protect eyes only.
  • Agree on a signal. Your child waves or taps if a plug slips or an ear hurts.

🏊 Step 3: Tell the Swim School

  • Share the ENT’s advice in writing with the front desk and the instructor before class one.
  • Use a medical information form so the note stays with your child’s record.
  • Ask how skills are adapted such as submersion, bobbing and diving games.
  • Name one contact who gets a call if your child has ear pain at the pool.

🚦 Step 4: Go or Skip the Lesson

What you seePlan
Fluid or pus draining from the earSkip the lesson and call the doctor
Ear pain or tugging at the earSkip and call the doctor
Fever or feeling unwellSkip and rest
Plug fell out or will not stay inPause, refit, ask your ENT if needed
No symptoms, ENT approval in handGo, with the gear and notes above
When in doubt, call your doctor. Do not guess about drainage or pain. A missed class is easy to make up; an untreated ear problem is not.

🚿 Step 5: After the Lesson

  • Dry the outer ear gently with a clean towel and follow your ENT’s advice.
  • Remove and rinse plugs and let them dry before the next class.
  • Watch for the next day fluid, redness, a fever or a child who pulls at the ear.
  • Tell the instructor what you learned so next week’s plan is better.

This checklist is a family planner, not medical advice and not a substitute for supervision or emergency training. Always follow your child’s ENT or pediatrician. In an emergency call 911. Guidance: American Academy of Pediatrics and CDC drowning prevention.
