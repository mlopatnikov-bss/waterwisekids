Swim Lesson Lost and Found Gear Card

Goggles, towels and swimsuits look alike. Label inside, pack with a photo, sweep before you leave and act fast if something goes missing.

🏷️ Step 2: Label It Safely

  • Inside, not outside Use a care tag, waistband or fabric tape, not a big name on the front.
  • Initials and phone Initials or a house name plus a parent phone number.
  • Laundry-proof marker Use permanent laundry ink, iron-on tags or wet-gear stickers.
  • Bright bag, fun symbol Let your child spot their own bag without a name.
  • Case labels Mark goggle cases, ear plug cases and prescription goggles with your phone number.
  • Re-check each season Refresh faded labels and label new gear before the first class.

📸 Step 3: Pack and Photo

  • Pack together Say each item aloud with your child.
  • Take a photo Snap the laid-out gear and keep it on your phone.
  • Mesh pouch Zip goggles, ear plugs and small items in one pouch.
  • Adult-held items Keep medicines, phones and valuables with an adult, never alone in a bag.

🔍 Step 4: End-of-Class Sweep

  • Bench or hook Check where the bag and towel were set down.
  • Deck or cubby Check where your child waited and put on shoes.
  • Changing area Check with a parent or staff present. Never send a child in alone.
  • Compare to the photo Have your child point to each item.
  • Count at the door Count the bag contents one last time before leaving.
  • Nobody leaves early Everyone stays together until the sweep is done.
Tip: do the sweep while your child is calm and dressed. A rushed exit is when goggles get left behind.

📦 Step 5: Lost Item Recovery Log

  • Tell the front desk Describe the item, color and label, and leave a phone number.
  • Check four places Lost and found, locker room, pool deck and your car.
  • Ask the policy How long are items kept, and when are they donated?
  • Check at next class Look again and write down what you find.
  • Do not reuse found gear Wash or replace goggles, caps, ear plugs and swimsuits first.

Children are never alone in or near water. Searching for gear never comes before supervision. If a child is in trouble in the water, get help and call 911 or your local emergency number. Lessons do not make a child drown-proof, at any age.
