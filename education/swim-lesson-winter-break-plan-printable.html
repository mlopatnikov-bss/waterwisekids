Swim Lesson Winter Break Plan

Lessons pausing for the holidays? Write down the dates, choose how to handle the gap, practice safely at home and plan the return. This page puts the break plan in one place so skills stay comfortable and supervision stays tight.

WaterWiseKids.com — Free water safety education for families

📅 Step 1: The Basics

Swimmer’s name, level and class day and time
School and instructor
Last class before the break (date)

Do this first. Ask the school for its holiday closure dates and write them here. Dates and policies differ by school, so follow your program’s written schedule.

❓ Step 2: Questions to Ask the School

  • Are any classes closed for the holidays? Write the dates in Step 1.
  • Can a missed or closed class be made up? Ask by when and how to book.
  • Is my child’s spot held between sessions? Ask what it costs, if anything.
  • Is there a credit, freeze or refund for the gap? Ask for it in writing.
  • Can we visit for a family swim or a trial class during the break? Ask what is offered and the rules.
  • What should we practice, and what should we leave for class? Ask the instructor for two or three ideas.

🔁 Step 3: Our Break Choice

ChoiceDatesCost or creditChosen?
Pause for the break

Remember. There is no single right choice. A young child still building basic skills may do better with a shorter gap, and a tired family may need the rest.

🛁 Step 4: Safe, Simple Practice at Home

  • Blow bubbles in a bowl or the bath. Keep it short and playful.
  • Wet the face at a comfortable pace. Never push or hold a child under.
  • Kick while sitting at the edge of the tub. Only with an adult in reach.
  • Sing the class songs. Familiar routines help.
  • An adult stays within arm’s reach in the bath. No phone, no leaving the room, no sibling in charge.
  • Stop when it stops being fun. A few minutes is plenty.
Bath play is not pool practice. It can keep water on the face feeling normal, but it does not replace lessons or supervision.

🧳 Step 5: Holiday Travel and Gatherings

  • Name a water watcher for every pool or hot tub. Write the name below and pass the job on out loud.
  • Check gates, fences and covers on arrival. Keep them closed and latched.
  • Keep young children within arm’s reach. A pause in lessons is not a pause in supervision.
  • Use a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket near open water. Water wings and toys are not life jackets.
  • Find out where the nearest phone and flotation device are. Know who calls 911.
Where we will be near water (hotel, rental, relatives)
Water watcher on each day

🔙 Step 6: Return-to-Class Plan

  • Tell the instructor how long the gap was. Share what you practiced.
  • Let my child watch for a few minutes if needed. Do not force it.
  • Start with comfortable skills. Build back up over a few classes.
  • Pack the cold-weather routine. Towel, warm clothes and a plan to get home dry.
  • Give it three or four classes before judging progress. Note changes below.

A plan is one layer, not the only one. Close, undistracted supervision, a designated water watcher, barriers around pools and a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket near open water all matter. Lessons and home practice do not make a child drown-proof, at any age.

✅ Step 8: Our Family Agreement

We agree to this plan (names)
Adult who will review it with the children
Date we will review it again
Changes after the first class back
A line to remember: “Pause the lessons, never the supervision.” Write the dates, keep practice short and safe, and stay close to the water.
