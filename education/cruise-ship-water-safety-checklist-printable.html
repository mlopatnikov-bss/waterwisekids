Cruise Ship Water Safety Checklist

For families cruising with children. Many cruise pools have no lifeguard, so plan who is watching, check life jackets, lock the balcony and keep the same habits on shore days.

WaterWiseKids.com — Free water safety education for families

📋 Step 1: Our Cruise Details

Ship and sailing dates
Cabin number
Ship emergency number
Muster station
Family meeting spot
Kids’ club hours and water rules

🧳 Step 2: Before We Sail

  • Pool rules checked. I know which pools have lifeguards and the slide age and height rules.
  • Swim diaper policy checked. I know where babies and toddlers may and may not swim.
  • Life jackets packed. Each non-swimmer or weak swimmer has a fitted U.S. Coast Guard approved life jacket, not floaties.
  • Swim skills noted. I wrote down what each child can really do in deep water.
  • Sun and water gear packed. Sunscreen, rash guards, hats and water bottles are in the bag.

🕐 Step 3: First Hour on Board

  • Walked the pool decks. We found every pool, slide, splash area and hot tub and noted depths and lifeguards.
  • Checked cabin life jackets. There are sizes that fit our children, or I asked the cabin steward.
  • Saved the emergency number. It is written in Step 1 and in my phone.
  • Locked the balcony. Chairs and tables are away from the railing.
  • Picked a meeting spot. Every child knows where to go if we get separated.

🏊 Step 4: Pool Day Water Watcher Schedule

One named adult watches at a time, with no phone. Swap every 15 to 30 minutes and say “you’re the watcher now.”

🛑 Step 5: Railing and Balcony Rules

  • No climbing, sitting or leaning on railings. Not even for a photo, and no lifting children up.
  • Balcony door locked unless an adult is out there with the children.
  • Hands held on open decks for small children, especially in wind or crowds.

🏝️ Step 6: Shore Excursion Check

  • Lifeguarded beach when possible. We swim near the guard and check the warning flags.
  • Life jackets for snorkeling and boats. I asked the tour operator about kids’ sizes before booking.
  • Same watcher habit on shore. One adult is named as the water watcher every time.

🆘 Step 7: If a Child Is Missing or in Trouble

  • Check the water first. Look in and around pools, hot tubs and splash areas.
  • Shout and alert crew right away. Call the ship’s emergency number.
  • Reach or throw, don’t go. Get help instead of jumping in, and have the ship’s medical team check any child who had trouble in the water.
A cruise pool may have no lifeguard. Even where one is posted, a guard is a backup, not a babysitter. An adult watches every time children are in or near water; children never supervise each other.

This checklist is a family planner, not medical advice. Always follow your cruise line’s rules and crew instructions. Guidance: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention; American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org); U.S. Coast Guard Boating Safety; American Red Cross.
