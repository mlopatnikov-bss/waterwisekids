Swim Lesson Gift Checklist: What to Ask Before You Give Lessons (Free Printable)

Swim lessons can be one of the most useful gifts a child receives, but only if they fit the child and the family’s schedule. Here is how to pick the class, ask the school the right questions and make the gift easy to use, plus a free one-page checklist.

Quick Answer: Before giving lessons, check with the parents, match the class to the child’s age and experience, ask the school about gift, transfer, expiry and make-up policies, and confirm start dates that fit the family. Add a swimsuit, goggles and a towel to make it a complete gift. Our free printable puts every question on one page.

🎁 Why Swim Lessons Make a Good Gift

Toys fade; skills last. Swim lessons build water confidence, breath control and the basic skills that help children stay safer around water, and they are something a family may not have budgeted for. Lessons do not replace supervision (see the Water Watcher card), but they are one important layer of protection. Our guide to swimming as a life skill explains why.

💬 Start With the Parents

Before you buy anything, ask the parents. They know the child’s schedule, comfort in water, health needs and budget, and they will be the ones bringing the child each week. Ask which days and times work, whether the child has had lessons before, and whether any health or sensory needs affect class choice. If the child has ear tubes, see our ear tubes parent checklist; for sensory or developmental needs, read special needs water safety.

🏊 Match the Class to the Child

Age and experience decide the level. Babies and toddlers usually start in parent-and-child classes, preschoolers in small group classes, and school-age children are placed by skill. Ask the school for a placement or short assessment, and use our level placement guide and best age for swim lessons to know what to expect. A first month guide helps parents plan.

❓ Ask the School Before You Buy

Policies differ by school, so ask and get answers in writing: how gift purchases or pre-paid lessons work, whether they can be transferred to another child, when they expire, how make-up classes are handled and what the cancellation terms are. Our policy fine print checklist and questions to ask a swim school cover what to look for. If you are comparing programs, start with the enrollment checklist and choosing a swim school.

🧳 Add a Few Practical Extras

A well-fitting swimsuit, goggles, a towel and a swim bag turn a voucher into a gift a child can open. See the swim bag checklist and gear fit checklist. Avoid giving inflatable floaties as a substitute for lessons or supervision; read why they are not safety devices.

🎀 Make It Easy to Use

The best gift is one that gets used. Include the school’s contact details, the start date and a note with the class day and time. For a surprise, add a simple card the child can open. Help the family plan cold-weather lesson days with our pool-to-car routine card, and if cost is a concern for a family you know, point them to swim lesson scholarships.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a swim lesson a good holiday gift?

Often yes, because it gives a child a lifelong skill rather than another toy. It works best when the gift is paired with a plan: pick a program that fits the child's age, check start dates, and make sure the family can actually attend on a regular schedule.

What should I ask a swim school before buying lessons as a gift?

Ask how gift purchases or pre-paid lessons work, whether they can be transferred to another child, when they expire, what the start dates and class levels are, and how the school handles make-up classes. Policies differ by school, so get the answers in writing.

How do I choose the right class level for a child I'm gifting to?

Share the child's age and any current water experience with the school and ask for a level placement or a short assessment. Our swim lesson level placement guide explains common level names so you can ask the right questions.

Should I ask the parents before giving swim lessons?

Yes. Parents know the child's schedule, comfort in water, health needs and budget. A quick message avoids a gift that cannot be used, and lets the family pick the day and time.

What can I give alongside lessons?

Practical gear such as a well-fitting swimsuit, goggles, a towel and a swim bag makes the gift feel complete. Skip inflatable floaties as a substitute for supervision or lessons; they are not safety devices.

Can I give lessons to a baby or toddler?

Many schools offer parent-and-child classes for young babies and toddlers. The gift then also involves an adult who attends, so confirm the adult's availability and the class format first.

Do swim lessons make a child safe around water?

Lessons build skills and water confidence, but they do not make a child drown-proof. Constant adult supervision, barriers and a Water Watcher remain essential.

Can gifted lessons be used in winter?

Yes. Most indoor programs run year-round, and winter is a good time to keep skills going. Check the start date and make a plan for the pool-to-car routine in cold weather.
