Swim School Policy Fine Print: What to Ask Before You Enroll

Families compare tuition and instructors carefully, then sign the enrollment agreement without reading it. That agreement is what decides how much of the tuition buys lessons your child actually swims — and how much it costs to leave. 📋 What should you ask a swim school before enrolling?

Ask about six things the price will never tell you: enrollment model, makeups, freezes, cancellation, refunds and tiers. Families compare tuition and instructor credentials carefully and then sign the enrollment agreement without reading it, which is odd, because the agreement is what decides how much of that tuition buys lessons your child actually swims.

None of this is about catching anyone out. Swim schools schedule instructors and plan class ratios around enrolled students, so most of these terms exist for legitimate operational reasons. But they are almost never volunteered, and a policy you learn about in month seven is a policy you had no chance to weigh in month one. The six areas below are what our full guides to make-up lesson policies, pausing and freezing lessons and how hard it is to quit a swim school cover in depth — collected into a single page you can hold while somebody answers.

🔁 Which enrollment model are you actually in?

Ask this before you ask the price, because it changes what every other answer means. Perpetual enrollment holds your child a permanent slot and bills every month, year-round, until you give notice. Session-based programs sell fixed blocks, often eight to twelve weeks, and simply end.

Neither is the bad one. Year-round consistency genuinely teaches faster, which is the honest case for perpetual enrollment, and it is why our guide to calculating the true annual cost of lessons treats fifty-two weeks of billing as normal rather than sinister. Session programs let you stop without a conversation. They are different products, and makeup rules, freezes and notice periods only make sense once you know which one you are buying. Ask too whether autopay is required and whether you are signing month to month or a term — some agreements look monthly until a prepaid-package clause makes them a term.

🎫 Do makeup lessons expire, and does the number matter?

The number of makeups is the least informative part of a makeup policy. Common rules cap them at two to four per session, require use within 30 to 60 days, demand 24 to 48 hours of advance notice, and offer only whatever slots are already open.

Those four restrictions are the policy. As our guide to why “12 free makeups” is not twelve makeups sets out, a generous headline can yield a handful of usable lessons once expiry, notice, booking limits and availability are applied — and a four-makeup policy with no expiry and easy online booking can be worth more than a twelve-makeup one that is not. Expiry punishes exactly the families who need makeups most, because a fortnight of illness or a family trip can burn an allocation before any of it is rebookable. Ask whether a sibling can use a makeup, which matters a great deal with two children enrolled, and what happens to unused makeups when you leave. This is also why the makeup policy belongs in your budget: it changes the real cost per lesson attended more than the headline rate does.

⏸️ Can you pause lessons for a holiday?

Often yes — commonly up to four weeks per twelve-month period with two weeks’ notice, sometimes for a reduced hold fee rather than free. On perpetual billing a three-week trip otherwise means three full payments for an empty seat.

The question families forget is what the hold actually preserves. A freeze that keeps your enrollment but not your specific class slot can mean coming back to a different day, a different instructor, or a waitlist — which for an anxious child is not a small thing, as our guide to separation anxiety at swim lessons explains. Ask what the hold costs, whether it holds your exact slot or only your place in the school, and whether returning within a window, commonly 60 to 90 days, waives any re-enrollment fee. Ask before you book the holiday. Our full guide to freeze and hold policies walks through the three main variants schools use.

🚪 How do you leave, and what does leaving cost?

Thirty days’ written notice is the most common policy at perpetual-enrollment schools, so leaving usually costs one more month. That is reasonable on its own. The fine print around it is what deserves attention.

Two details do most of the damage. The first is method: the strictest published policies require a withdrawal form signed at the facility rather than an email, which is worth knowing while you are already standing in the building. The second is a clause making your withdrawal effective only after prepaid lessons are used — which quietly converts a month-to-month arrangement into a term contract. Ask in exactly those words, ask which date starts the thirty days, and ask whether pausing for a summer is treated as cancelling, because at some schools a long freeze ends enrollment and you rejoin as a new family, join fee and all. If you are already weighing a move, our switching swim schools checklist covers sequencing the exit so your child is not out of the water in between.

💳 Refund or credit — which are you being offered?

A refund returns money. A credit only holds value inside the school you are trying to leave. Many published policies issue account credit for missed or unused lessons and reserve real refunds for documented medical withdrawal.

Strictness is not automatically a red flag: a held slot is a genuine cost to a school that has scheduled an instructor around it, and a firm no-refund policy stated plainly is more honest than a vague promise. The care is warranted in one specific place — prepaying a large package into a policy that returns nothing. Prepay discounts are real, so match the size of the prepayment to how confident you are after a trial class, and ask what happens to your balance if the school closes or changes hands. Families paying by card retain one further lever: card networks allow disputes for services paid for and never delivered, typically within 60 to 120 days of the charge. Our guide to swim school refund policies maps the full spectrum, and the fees that never appear in the quote covers what else sits outside the headline.

⭐ Are membership tiers and add-ons worth it?

Only if you will use what the tier adds, and only if you can cancel it separately. Upgrades typically bundle a monthly private mini-lesson, gear discounts, priority scheduling or guest passes on top of standard tuition.

Price the bundle honestly: what would those items cost individually, and how many months will you realistically use them? A monthly private mini-lesson has real value for a child stuck at a level and much less for one moving steadily through a group class — and class size may matter more than either, which is why the instructor-to-student ratio is worth asking about in the same conversation. Then ask whether the upgrade unwinds on its own without restarting the enrollment conversation, and whether the tier price is locked or reviewed annually. Our guide to swim school membership tiers runs the worth-it arithmetic, and if you are comparing two schools overall, the swim school comparison worksheet handles the non-policy half of the decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

What questions should I ask a swim school before enrolling?

Ask about six things the price will never tell you. First, the enrollment model: perpetual year-round billing or fixed sessions. Second, makeup lessons: how many you get, whether they expire, how much notice you must give, and whether a sibling can use them. Third, freeze or hold: whether the school will pause billing for a trip, what it costs, and whether your specific class slot is held. Fourth, cancellation: the notice period, the method, and the exact date the clock starts. Fifth, refunds: whether unused lessons come back as money or only as account credit. Sixth, membership tiers: what an upgrade actually adds and whether it can be cancelled separately. Ask for the answers in writing, on the enrollment agreement, before you pay.

How many makeup lessons should a swim school offer?

A reasonable policy allows at least two makeups per enrollment session and banks them for at least 30 to 60 days. Common rules cap makeups at two to four per session, require them to be used within 30 to 60 days, demand 24 to 48 hours of advance notice, and restrict them to whatever slots happen to be open. The headline number matters far less than those four restrictions. A four-makeup policy with no expiry and easy online booking is worth more than a twelve-makeup policy where the tokens expire in a month and the only open slot is 8 a.m. on a Tuesday. Ask for the number and the four rules together, because the number alone is close to meaningless.

Do swim lesson makeup credits expire?

Usually yes. Typical expiration windows run 30 to 90 days from the missed lesson, or to the end of the current enrollment session, whichever comes first. That is the single restriction that decides whether a generous-sounding makeup allowance is real. Expiry punishes exactly the families who need makeups most: a two-week illness or a summer trip can burn through a whole allocation before any of it can be rebooked. When you ask how many makeups you get, ask in the same breath when they expire, how far in advance you must cancel to earn one, and whether unused makeups carry into the next session.

Can I pause or freeze swim lessons for vacation?

Many perpetual-enrollment schools allow it, but the terms vary widely and are rarely volunteered. Common terms are up to four weeks of freeze per twelve-month period with two weeks of advance notice, sometimes for a reduced hold fee rather than free. The question that matters is what the hold actually preserves. A freeze that holds your enrollment but not your specific class slot can mean returning to a different day, a different instructor, or a waitlist. Ask three things: what the hold costs, whether it holds your exact slot or only your place in the school, and whether returning within a window, commonly 60 to 90 days, waives any re-enrollment fee.

Is a 30-day cancellation notice normal for swim schools?

Yes. Thirty days' written notice is the most common policy at perpetual-enrollment swim schools, which means you typically pay for one more month after you give notice. That is not unreasonable on its own, because instructors are scheduled and class ratios are planned around enrolled students. What deserves attention is the fine print around it: whether notice must be a signed form delivered in person rather than an email, and whether a clause makes your withdrawal effective only after any prepaid lessons are used up. That second clause quietly converts a month-to-month arrangement into a term contract, so find it before you sign rather than after.

Do swim schools give refunds for unused lessons?

Often not in money. The distinction that matters is refund versus credit: a refund returns cash, while a credit only holds value inside the school you are trying to leave. Many published policies issue account credit for missed or unused lessons and reserve actual refunds for cases like medical withdrawal with documentation, and the strictest state plainly that once a spot is sold it cannot be resold, so no refunds are issued at all. Strict is not automatically a red flag, because a held slot is a genuine cost to the school. What is worth care is prepaying a large package into a policy that returns nothing. Families paying by card do keep one more lever: card networks allow disputes for services paid for and never delivered, typically within 60 to 120 days of the charge.

Are swim school membership tiers worth it?

Only if you will actually use what the tier adds, and only if you can leave it separately. Upgrade tiers typically bundle a monthly private mini-lesson, gear discounts, priority scheduling or guest passes on top of standard tuition. Price the bundle honestly: add up what you would pay for those items individually, and count how many months you will realistically use them. A monthly private mini-lesson has real value for a child who is stuck at a level and much less for one progressing steadily in a group. Then ask the question people forget, which is whether the upgrade can be cancelled on its own without disturbing the underlying enrollment, and whether the tier price is locked or reviewed annually.
