Year-Round Swim Skills Checklist

Swimming is a perishable skill — it fades over long breaks, fastest in new and nervous swimmers. Use this one-page checklist to spot fading skills, keep them sharp all year, and plan lessons so nothing your child worked for slips away.

WaterWiseKids.com — Free water safety education for families

📉 Signs Your Child's Skills May Be Fading

  • It's been more than a few weeks since your child's last swim lesson or supervised water practice.
  • Skills that were easy now look shaky — a float that wobbles, a breath mistimed, a hesitant wall return.
  • Confidence has slipped — a child who loved the water is suddenly clingy or anxious at the pool.
  • You're stuck repeating levels each year after a long "summer-only" break.

✅ How to Keep Swim Skills Sharp All Year

  • Keep lessons going year-round in warm, indoor water — the #1 way to prevent the long gap that erases skills.
  • Aim for consistency over intensity — weekly practice beats an intensive burst followed by months off.
  • Book the next term before the current one ends so there's no accidental break for skills to fade.
  • Track progress so you can see what's mastered and what needs a lesson's attention.

🏠 Between-Lesson Reinforcement (Touch-Supervised)

  • Practice the fundamentals during supervised pool or bath time: bubbles, back floats, reaching the wall, safe entries and exits.
  • Stay within arm's reach — active touch-supervision every second, with no distractions.
  • Keep it short, playful, and pressure-free — the goal is to keep skills warm and the water fun.
  • Remember it supports, not replaces, lessons — paddling a few feet is not drown-proofing.
