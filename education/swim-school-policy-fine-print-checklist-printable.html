Swim School Policy Fine-Print Checklist

Swim schools compete on price and instructors. What actually decides how much a year costs, and how easily you can leave, is the enrollment agreement — makeups, freezes, notice periods, refunds and tiers. Almost none of it comes up on a tour unless you ask. Take this with you and ask.

📝 Start Here — Before You Ask About Price

  • Ask for the enrollment agreement before you ask for the price. Every answer below should end up in writing on that document. A policy quoted only out loud is not a policy you can hold anyone to later.
  • Bring this on the tour, with someone in front of you who can answer. Reconstructing terms from a website two days later is how families end up guessing. Fill it in while the person who knows is standing there.
  • Use the School A and School B columns. A single completed checklist tells you very little. Two side by side tells you which school actually fits how your family lives.
  • Remember what you are buying. The point of all of this is that your child keeps swimming. A policy is only worth arguing about because consistent lessons are what build the skill.

🔁 1. Which Enrollment Model Are You In?

Ask this before anything else — it changes what every other answer means
AskWhy it mattersSchool ASchool B
“Is enrollment perpetual or session-based?”Perpetual bills year-round until you give notice. Session-based sells fixed blocks, often 8 to 12 weeks, and simply ends____________
“How many weeks a year do you bill?”Perpetual schools commonly bill all 52. The answer sets your real annual number____________
“Am I signing a term commitment or month to month?”Some agreements look monthly until a prepaid-package clause makes them a term____________
“Is autopay required, and can I turn it off?”Autopay plus a notice period is what makes leaving cost an extra month____________
Neither model is the bad one. Perpetual enrollment holds a permanent slot and keeps skills moving year-round, which genuinely teaches faster. Session-based programs let you stop without a conversation. They are simply different products, and the policies below only make sense once you know which one you are buying.

🎫 2. Makeup Lessons — the Number Is the Least Important Part

Ask all six. A headline number without these is not an answer
AskWhat you are listening forSchool ASchool B
“How many makeups do I get, and per what?”Per session, per year, or per child. Commonly two to four per session____________
“Do they expire, and when?”Typically 30 to 90 days, or end of session. This is the rule that decides everything____________
“How much notice must I give to earn one?”Often 24 to 48 hours. A sudden fever rarely gives you that____________
“How do I book one, and which slots are open?”Online self-serve, or a phone call into whatever is left midweek____________
“Can a sibling use my makeup?”Matters a lot with two or more children enrolled____________
“What happens to unused makeups if we leave?”Usually nothing. Worth knowing before you bank them____________
Twelve is not always more than four. A “12 free makeups” headline can yield only a handful of usable lessons once expiry windows, notice rules, booking limits and slot availability are applied. A four-makeup policy with no expiry and easy online booking can be worth more. Write down the number and the four restrictions, or you have not recorded the policy.

⏸️ 3. Freeze, Hold and Pause

For the summer trip you already know you are taking
AskWhat you are listening forSchool ASchool B
“Can I freeze billing, and for how long?”Common terms are up to four weeks per twelve-month period____________
“How far ahead must I request it?”Two weeks’ notice is a common requirement____________
“Is there a hold fee?”Some holds are free, some cost a reduced monthly rate____________
“Does the hold keep my exact class slot?”Or only my place in the school. This is the one people assume____________
“If I return within 60 to 90 days, is re-enrollment waived?”Otherwise a pause can cost a second join fee____________
Three weeks away, three payments. On perpetual billing a three-week trip means three full payments for an empty seat unless a freeze is agreed in advance. Ask before you book the holiday, not after.

🚪 4. Cancellation — How Do I Leave?

  • “What notice do I have to give, in days?” Thirty days’ written notice is the most common policy at perpetual schools, so leaving usually costs one more month. That is normal — just budget for it.
  • “Does an email count, or does it have to be a form?” The strictest published policies require a withdrawal form signed at the facility. Find out now, while you are already standing in it.
  • “When exactly does the 30 days start?” The date you send notice, the date they process it, or the start of the next billing cycle. Those are three different final bills.
  • “Does my notice start if I still have prepaid lessons?” This is the clause that turns month-to-month into a term. Ask it in exactly these words and write the answer down.
  • “Is pausing for the summer treated as cancelling?” At some schools a long freeze ends enrollment and you rejoin as a new family, join fee and all.
  • “Can you show me where that is written?” A friendly, completely reasonable question. Any school confident in its terms will simply point at the paragraph.
Hunt for one clause in particular. “Withdrawal effective after prepaid lessons are used” means your notice period does not begin until the package runs out. A month-to-month arrangement has quietly become a term contract. It is not hidden — it is simply never mentioned unless you read for it.

💳 5. Refund or Credit?

  • “If we stop mid-month, is anything refunded?” Often nothing, and that is a legitimate policy. You just want to know it before you pay rather than after.
  • “Is that a refund or an account credit?” A refund is money back. A credit only holds value inside the school you are trying to leave. This is the distinction that matters most.
  • “Do credits expire?” A credit with a short expiry attached to a school you have left is worth close to nothing.
  • “What happens if we withdraw for medical reasons?” Many otherwise strict policies carve out documented medical withdrawal. Ask what documentation they want.
  • “What happens to my prepayment if the school closes or is sold?” Ownership changes happen. It is a fair question and the answer tells you how much to prepay.
  • Think twice before prepaying a year into a no-refund policy. Prepay discounts are real, but so is the risk. Match the size of the prepayment to how confident you are after a trial class.
Strict is not automatically wrong. A held slot is a real cost to a school that has scheduled an instructor around it, and a firm no-refund policy that is clearly published is more honest than a vague promise. The thing to be careful about is prepaying a large package into a policy that returns nothing.

⭐ 6. Membership Tiers and Add-Ons

  • “What does the upgrade tier actually add?” Commonly a monthly private mini-lesson, gear discounts, priority scheduling or guest passes. Price those individually before you agree.
  • “How many months will we realistically use it?” A monthly private lesson is valuable for a child stuck at a level, much less so for one already progressing in a group.
  • “Can I cancel the tier without cancelling the lessons?” The question people forget. Some upgrades unwind separately; some restart the whole enrollment conversation.
  • “Is the tier price locked, or reviewed annually?” You are budgeting a year. An unmodelled annual increase is a real line item.
  • “How often do you raise tuition, and when did you last?” Ask it about the base rate too, not just the tier.

🚩 Answers Worth a Second Look

  • “We’ll sort that out if it comes up.” Policy questions have policy answers. Vagueness now is a negotiation later, at a moment when you have no leverage.
  • A fee or rule quoted only out loud. Anything that affects what you pay should be on the enrollment agreement before you sign it.
  • Pressure to enroll today for a rate that expires tonight. A checklist filled in calmly beats a discount that requires you not to fill one in.
  • A generous makeup headline with no written restrictions offered. The restrictions exist. If nobody will state them, that is the answer to the question.
  • Reluctance to show you the paragraph you asked about. Not proof of anything wrong — but it is the moment to slow down and read the whole document yourself.

The Fine Print Is Worth an Hour. The Lessons Are Worth More.

Reading the agreement carefully is how you avoid paying for months your child never swam. Then enroll and keep going: participation in formal swim lessons is associated with a large reduction in drowning risk for young children, and drowning remains a leading cause of death for children ages 1–4.
