Swim Lesson Annual Cost Worksheet

Swim schools quote the smallest number they have — usually a weekly one — and then bill monthly, all year. This worksheet turns that headline into the only two figures that compare honestly between schools: what twelve months actually costs your family, and what one lesson your child attends actually costs.

📝 Start Here — Before You Fill Anything In

  • Write the advertised price exactly as they said it. “$35 a week.” “$150 a month.” “$260 for the session.” You will want it at the end, to see how far the real number moved.
  • Ask which billing model they use, before anything else. Perpetual means you stay enrolled and are billed year-round until you cancel. Session-based means you pay per block of weeks and it simply ends. This one answer drives more of the annual total than the price does.
  • Ask how many weeks they actually bill in a year. Perpetual schools commonly bill all 52. Session programs might bill 16 or 30. Multiplying a weekly rate by the wrong number of weeks is the most common way families underestimate.
  • Print two copies and fill one per school. Or use the School A / School B columns below. Comparing two completed worksheets is the whole point — a single number in isolation tells you very little.

The makeup policy is a price, not a perk. Under perpetual billing you pay for every week whether or not your child swims. A school with generous, non-expiring makeups can cost meaningfully less per lesson attended than a school whose headline rate is lower but whose missed lessons simply vanish. Work out the per-lesson line before you decide which school is the cheaper one.

🔎 Five Questions That Change the Total

  • “What is my notice period to cancel, and how do I give it?” A 30-day written window means leaving costs you one more month. Find out whether an email counts or it has to be a form.
  • “What happens to our spot and our rate if we pause for the summer?” Some schools hold the slot free, some charge a hold fee, and some re-charge the enrollment fee when you come back.
  • “How often do you raise tuition, and when did you last?” You are budgeting a full year. An annual increase you did not model is a real line item.
  • “How many children are in the class, and what is the instructor-to-student ratio?” Cost per lesson means little until you know how much of the lesson your child actually gets.
  • “How long does a typical child take to reach the next level here?” The real question is not what a year costs — it is what water competency costs. A pricier school that gets there sooner can total less.

🚩 Pricing Answers Worth a Second Look

  • “It’s just $35 a week” — with no annual figure offered. Not dishonest, but it is a framing choice. Ask for the twelve-month number and notice whether they have it ready.
  • A fee schedule that is not in writing. Anything quoted only out loud should be on the enrollment agreement before you sign it.
  • “We’ll sort the cancellation out when you get there.” Get the notice period, the method and the deadline in writing on day one, while you still have leverage.
  • Pressure to enroll today for a rate that expires tonight. A worksheet filled in calmly beats a discount that requires you not to.
Cheapest is not the goal. A higher price often buys warm water, small classes, year-round consistency and real makeup flexibility — things that can genuinely speed learning, especially for young or anxious children. Municipal and YMCA session programs can total a fraction of a perpetual school’s year, usually in larger classes and cooler water. Both can be the right call for a family. You can only judge the value once the numbers are honest.

The Worksheet Prices the Lessons. The Lessons Are Still the Point.

Cost is worth comparing carefully — and then it is worth spending. Participation in formal swim lessons is associated with a large reduction in drowning risk for young children, and drowning remains a leading cause of death for children ages 1–4. Once you know the honest number, find the program that gets your child to water competency.
