Questions to Ask Your Child's Swim Instructor

Most parents watch lessons from the bleachers and never ask a single question — then quietly wonder whether their child is progressing. Two minutes at the edge of the pool answers that. Circle the questions that matter most to you and bring this to your next lesson.

🗓️ Set Up the Conversation

  • Ask at the end of class, not the start — the instructor is off the clock for teaching and has just watched your child swim.
  • Keep it to two or three questions — pick the ones you most need answered rather than the whole list.
  • Ask for a longer check-in if you need one — most schools will schedule a five-minute progress conversation.
  • Lead with curiosity, not complaint — “help me understand what you're working on” opens far more than “why isn't she moving up?”
  • Write the answer down — you will not remember it by the drive home.

📈 Progress: What Is My Child Actually Working On?

  • “What one skill are you working on with my child right now?” A good instructor can name it in a sentence.
  • “What does mastering that skill look like?” Ask for the observable version — how many seconds, how many feet, how independently.
  • “What has improved since last month?” Progress often shows up as comfort and control long before it shows as a new level.
  • “What is the thing holding my child back most?” Usually it is breath control, fear, or body position — each has a different fix.
  • “Is what I'm seeing from the bleachers normal?” Lessons often look like play; ask what the play is teaching.

🪜 Advancement: What Gets Us to the Next Level?

  • “What exactly does my child need to demonstrate to move up?” Ask for the written criteria if the school has them.
  • “How close are we — weeks, or months?” A rough honest range beats a vague “soon.”
  • “Does the school advance on mastery or on session length?” Mastery-based programs move a child when the skill is real, not when the calendar says so.
  • “Who decides, and how often is progress assessed?” You should know who evaluates and on what schedule.
  • “Would a different class time, level, or instructor serve my child better?” Ask it plainly — good schools welcome it.

🛟 Safety: Could My Child Help Themselves?

  • “If my child fell in fully clothed today, what would happen?” This is the single most useful question a parent can ask.
  • “Can my child roll over, float, and breathe unassisted?” Back-floating and breath recovery are survival skills, not stroke skills.
  • “Can my child get back to the wall or the edge on their own?” Entering, turning, and returning is core water competency.
  • “What can my child NOT yet do safely?” The honest answer tells you where supervision still has to be constant.
  • Remember: no lesson makes a child drown-proof. Swim skill is one layer of protection alongside barriers, life jackets, and eyes-on supervision.

🏡 At Home: What Should We Practice?

  • “What is one thing we can practice between lessons?” Ask for something you can do in a bathtub or a shallow pool.
  • “What should I avoid doing?” Well-meaning parent coaching can undo an instructor's work — especially around breath-holding and floaties.
  • “How should I respond when my child is scared or refuses?” Get the instructor's script and use the same words at home.
  • “How much open swim time helps?” Unstructured pool time often accelerates comfort more than extra drilling.

The Right Program Answers These Questions Gladly

An instructor who can tell you exactly what your child is working on, what mastery looks like, and what would happen if your child fell in — that is what a quality, safety-first program sounds like. Formal swim lessons reduce drowning risk by up to 88% for children ages 1–4.
