Renting a Private Pool by the Hour: A Safety Guide for Families

Booking a stranger's backyard pool for an afternoon is one of summer's most popular new conveniences. It can be a wonderful day — as long as you treat an unfamiliar, unguarded pool with the caution it deserves.

Quick Answer: A pool you rent by the hour has no lifeguard, and you've never seen it before, so you are 100 percent responsible for safety. On arrival, confirm the water is clear, drain covers are intact, the fence and gate work, and you know the deep end. Then assign a dedicated water watcher, put non-swimmers in Coast Guard-approved life jackets, and keep your phone unlocked with the property address ready for a 911 call.

Why did pool rentals take off, and what changes for safety?

Pool-rental apps took off for the privacy and convenience — but the moment you book a backyard pool, you take on every safety role a lifeguard and maintenance crew would normally cover. Apps that let homeowners rent out their backyard pools by the hour have exploded in popularity. For families without a pool, they offer a private, affordable alternative to a crowded public pool: book a slot, show up, and have the water to yourselves.

The convenience is real, but so is a quiet shift in responsibility. At a public pool, trained lifeguards and a maintenance staff handle safety. At a rented backyard pool, there is no lifeguard, no posted safety staff, and no guarantee the pool meets the standards you would expect. You are walking into an unfamiliar environment and taking on every safety role yourself. The mindset is closer to renting a vacation home with a pool than to visiting a community facility.

Why this matters: According to the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), drowning is the leading cause of unintentional injury death for children ages 1 to 4, and most young-child drownings happen in residential pools — exactly the kind you rent by the hour. An unfamiliar private pool combines the highest-risk setting with the lowest level of professional oversight.

What should you check before you book?

Before you book, confirm the listing has a four-sided fence, an appropriate shallow end, on-site safety equipment, recent maintenance, and clear photos of the whole pool. Good safety starts in the listing, before you ever arrive. As you browse, look for and ask about:

  • A four-sided fence separating the pool from the house, especially if you have young children.
  • Shallow-end depth appropriate for your kids' heights and abilities.
  • Safety equipment on site — a reaching pole, ring buoy, or shepherd's crook.
  • Recent maintenance — ask the host when the pool was last serviced and balanced.
  • Clear photos showing the whole pool, the deck, and the fencing, not just a flattering corner.

If a host cannot answer basic safety questions, that is useful information. Choose a different listing.

What should your on-arrival safety walk-through cover?

Before a single child gets in, take five minutes to confirm you can see the bottom, the drain covers are intact, the fence and gate work, you know the deep and shallow ends, and help is reachable. Before a single child gets in the water, take five minutes to inspect the pool yourself. This walk-through is the most important thing you will do all day.

Can you see the bottom?

Clear water lets you see the main drain at the deepest point. If the water is cloudy or green, do not let anyone swim — not only is it a sign of poor maintenance, but you would not be able to see a child who slipped under. Our pool chemistry basics for parents explains what healthy water should look and smell like.

Are the drain covers intact?

Look at the bottom and walls for drain covers. They should be domed and firmly attached, not flat, cracked, or missing. A faulty drain can trap a child underwater — the entrapment hazard detailed in our pool drain safety guide. Keep children away from all drains and suction outlets.

Do the fence and gate work?

If there is a fence, confirm the gate self-closes and latches. Note any furniture a child could climb. If there is no barrier between the pool and the rest of the yard, your supervision has to be even tighter.

Where are the deep and shallow ends?

Walk the perimeter, note depth markings, and find any sudden drop-offs. Point them out to your children before they get in. Identify the nearest exit and the steps or ladder.

Is help reachable?

Confirm the exact property address so you can give it to 911, keep a charged phone unlocked and poolside, and locate the nearest spot where emergency vehicles could arrive.

How do you stay safe during your rental?

During the rental, assign one undistracted water watcher, keep non-swimmers in approved life jackets, enforce the buddy system, and keep rescue equipment within reach — because there is no lifeguard. With the pool checked, your job shifts to relentless supervision for the length of the booking. Because there is no lifeguard, the rules are simple and non-negotiable.

Assign one adult as the water watcher whose only job is to watch the swimmers — no phone, no chatting, no grilling. Rotate the role on a timer so no one gets fatigued or distracted. Keep young children and weak swimmers in properly fitted, U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jackets, not water wings (see our life jacket guide). Enforce the buddy system, ban running and rough play on the deck, and keep a reaching pole within grabbing distance of the water.

Think of all of this as applying the five layers of protection to a pool that came with none of them pre-installed. You are supplying the barriers, the supervision, the life jackets, and the emergency plan yourself — and that is exactly what makes a rented pool a safe and happy way to spend an afternoon.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is renting a private pool by the hour safe for kids?

It can be, but a rented backyard pool has no lifeguard and you have never seen it before, so you are fully responsible for safety. Inspect the pool, fencing, drains, and water clarity on arrival, supervise constantly, and use life jackets for non-swimmers.

What should I check when I arrive at a rented pool?

Check that you can see the bottom clearly, that drain covers are intact, that the fence and gate work, that you know the deep and shallow ends, and that safety equipment and an unlocked phone are on hand. Locate the nearest exit and confirm the property address for emergency calls.

Are there lifeguards at rented private pools?

No. Pool rentals are private backyard pools with no lifeguard on duty. The supervising adults in your group are the only safety net, so assign a dedicated water watcher for the entire rental.

How do I know the water is clean at a pool rental?

You should be able to see the main drain clearly through the water. Cloudy, green, or strongly chemical-smelling water is a red flag for poor maintenance. If you cannot see the bottom, do not let children swim, because you also could not see a child in trouble.

What if the rented pool has no fence?

Many backyard rentals lack a four-sided fence between the house and pool. If young children are present, this raises the risk significantly, so maintain unbroken touch supervision, keep doors to the pool area closed, and consider whether the listing is appropriate for your child's age and swimming ability.
