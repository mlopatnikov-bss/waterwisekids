Swim Lessons with Ear Tubes: A Parent Checklist (Free Printable)

Ear tubes do not have to mean skipping swim lessons, but they do mean a few questions to settle first. Here is what to ask your ENT, how to choose plugs and gear, what to tell the instructor, which warning signs mean skip the lesson, and a free one-page checklist.

Quick Answer: Ask your child's ENT before the first lesson whether plugs are needed and whether there are limits on diving or deep submersion, tell the swim school in writing, check plug fit every class, and skip the lesson and call your doctor if you see drainage, ear pain or fever. Our free printable puts the whole plan on one page.

👂 Ear Tubes and Swim Lessons

Ear tubes (tympanostomy tubes) are tiny tubes placed in the eardrum, usually to help with repeated ear infections or fluid. Many families worry that swim lessons are off the table afterwards. Medical guidance has evolved and many ENT doctors are comfortable with pool swimming, but advice differs by child, so your doctor’s word comes first. Read our full guide on swimming with ear tubes, and this page for the practical lesson-day plan. This article is general education, not medical advice.

🩺 Questions to Ask Your ENT

Before the first lesson, ask: Does my child need ear plugs in the pool, at the beach or in lakes? Are there limits on diving, going deep or putting the head fully underwater? How soon after surgery can lessons start? What symptoms mean I should call you? Write the answers on the printable and share them with the instructor. If your child has had recent ear infections, our swimmer’s ear prevention checklist and sick-day swim lesson decision guide help with the go or skip decision.

🧢 Plugs, Caps and Gear

If your ENT recommends plugs, choose a style made for swimming and test the fit at home in the bath. Goggles protect eyes only; see swim goggles for kids and our gear fit checklist. Pack spare plugs in the swim bag and a towel for gently drying the outer ear. A plug that falls out mid-lesson is a good reason to agree on a signal with your child and instructor.

🏊 What to Tell the Instructor

Tell the front desk and instructor before the first class: your child has ear tubes, what the ENT advised about plugs and diving, and who to call about ear pain. Our swim lesson medical information form keeps the details in one place. Good programs adapt skills such as submersion and record medical notes; ask about this using the enrollment checklist and pool tour checklist. For families in Pennsylvania and New Jersey, our pages on British Swim School in the Jersey Shore area and Northwest Philadelphia point to the official location pages, where each location can confirm how it handles medical notes, or browse swim lessons near you.

🚿 Warning Signs and After the Lesson

Skip the lesson and call your doctor if there is drainage from the ear, ear pain, fever or your child seems unwell. After class, follow your ENT’s advice, dry the outer ear gently and watch for fluid, redness or a child pulling at the ear. Our post-lesson skin, hair and eye care checklist covers the rinse and dry routine. Watch fear too: if a child avoids the water after an ear scare, see how to help a child overcome fear of water.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my child take swim lessons with ear tubes?

Many children with ear tubes can swim in a well-maintained pool, but the answer depends on your child's ear history and tube type. Ask your ENT (ear, nose and throat) doctor before the first lesson and write down exactly what they say so you can share it with the instructor.

Does my child need ear plugs for swim lessons?

Some ENT doctors say plugs are optional for surface pool swimming and recommend them for diving, lakes or the ocean; others prefer them for every swim. Ask your ENT which they recommend for your child, and choose a plug that fits well so it stays in during lessons.

What should I tell the swim instructor about ear tubes?

Tell the instructor and front desk before the first class: that your child has ear tubes, whether the ENT wants plugs, any limits on diving or going deep underwater, and who to call if your child complains of ear pain. Our medical information form has a place for this.

When should my child skip a swim lesson with ear tubes?

Skip the lesson and call your doctor if there is drainage from the ear, ear pain, fever or your child seems unwell. Do not wait for the next class to mention it; the ENT can tell you when it is fine to return.

Are goggles or a swim cap enough to protect ears?

No. Goggles cover the eyes and a regular cap does not seal the ear canal. Ask your ENT whether plugs, a snug headband or a neoprene cap is right for your child, and check the fit before every lesson.

What should I do after a swim lesson with ear tubes?

Follow your ENT's advice. Many families remove plugs, towel-dry the outer ear gently and watch for drainage or discomfort over the next day. Tell your doctor if you see fluid, redness or your child pulls at the ear.

Can kids with ear tubes dive or go deep underwater in lessons?

Ask your ENT. Some doctors advise avoiding deep diving or pushing the head far below the surface; others allow normal pool play. Tell your instructor about any limit so skills such as submersion are adapted.

Does ear tube care change which swim school I choose?

It can. Look for a program that listens to medical notes, adapts skills when needed and keeps a written record of your child's needs. Our enrollment checklist and pool tour checklist include the questions to ask.
