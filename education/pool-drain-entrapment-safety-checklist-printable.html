Pool Drain & Suction Entrapment Safety Checklist

Powerful drain suction can hold a child underwater in seconds. Compliant covers, a known pump shutoff, and a clear emergency plan prevent it. Work through this checklist before the season and after any pump or drain work.

🌀 Know the Four Entrapment Hazards

  • Suction entrapment: drain suction pulls a swimmer against the cover and holds them there — strongest with a single main drain and a powerful pump.
  • Body & limb entrapment: a small child, arm, or leg gets wedged in or pulled into a drain opening.
  • Hair entrapment: long hair wraps around drain components and pulls tighter — secure long hair in a bun or braid near drains.
  • Highest-risk spots: hot tubs and spas, wading pools, and any pool built before 2007 with an original single main drain.

🛠️ Confirm the Drain Is Compliant (VGB Act)

  • ANSI-certified anti-entrapment covers on every drain — engineered, not flat grates, with slots too small to catch hair (typically under 0.375").
  • A secondary safety system: a second drain at least as large as the main, an anti-suction cover, and/or an automatic pump shutoff (SVRS).
  • Older or home pools: retrofit single-main-drain pools with compliant covers or an automatic shutoff — meet Virginia Graeme Baker Act standards voluntarily.
  • Spas & hot tubs: same standards apply — jets add suction, so multiple drains and compliant covers matter even more.

🔎 Inspect Before Every Season

  • Look at every cover: present, intact, no cracks, no corrosion, no missing pieces.
  • Test attachment: gently pull each cover — it must not move or lift. Replace any loose or damaged cover immediately.
  • Locate the pump shutoff and make sure it is clearly labeled and reachable in seconds.
  • Book an annual professional inspection; at a public pool, ask staff when drains were last inspected and whether covers are compliant.

🚨 If Someone Is Trapped — Act in This Order

  • 1. Turn off the pump immediately — this stops the suction and is the single most important action.
  • 2. Call 911 and say someone is entrapped at a pool drain so responders arrive prepared.
  • Do NOT yank the person free — pulling against suction can cause serious injury. Break the suction first by shutting off the pump.
  • Keep them calm; be ready to give CPR if they stop breathing before help arrives.
