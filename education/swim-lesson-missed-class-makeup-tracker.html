Missed a Swim Lesson? How Make-Up Rules Work (and a Free Tracker)

Every family misses a swim class sooner or later, and whether you get a make-up depends on your school’s rules, not on luck. Here are the common types of make-up policy, the questions to ask before you enroll, when to skip for illness, and a free one-page tracker so no missed class or deadline slips through.

Quick Answer: Make-up rules vary by swim school: some give a make-up class, some a credit or token, and some nothing. Ask for the policy in writing before you enroll, write the deadline next to every missed class, and keep your child home when they are sick. Our free printable tracker logs each missed class, the reason, the school’s rule and the make-up deadline.

📅 Why Missed Swim Lessons Matter

Swim lessons work through repetition. A child who attends every week builds skills class by class, and a missed lesson is a gap in that chain. One gap is rarely a problem. Several in a row can mean a child returns a little less confident than they left. The point of a make-up is not to “get your money’s worth”; it is to keep your child’s time in the water steady. Our guide on why swim skills fade explains what happens during longer breaks.

🧾 What Types of Make-Up Policy Do Swim Schools Have?

Policies differ a lot, but most fall into a few patterns. Some schools let you book a make-up class within a set window. Others give a credit or token that you redeem later, sometimes with limits on levels, days or locations. Some offer a make-up only for illness or facility closures, and some do not offer make-ups for missed classes at all, especially for group lessons that are priced per session. Our article on swim school make-up lesson policies compares these in more detail, and make-up tokens decoded explains what “free make-ups” can and cannot mean.

❓ What Should You Ask Before You Enroll?

The time to learn the policy is before you pay. Ask how many make-ups are included, whether illness, weather and facility closures are treated differently, how far ahead you must give notice, how long a make-up stays valid, how you book one, and whether it can be used on a different day or at another location. Also ask what happens when the school cancels a class. Then write the answers on your tracker. Our swim school policy fine print checklist and cancellation policies guide cover the rest of the fine print.

🤒 When Should You Skip a Lesson for Illness?

Keep your child home if they have diarrhea, a fever or other signs of illness. The CDC advises that children with diarrhea should not swim and should wait at least 24 hours after it has stopped, and longer if Cryptosporidium is the cause. This protects the other families in the water, and it is a good reason to know your school’s illness policy before you need it. Many schools handle illness absences differently from other absences, but rules vary, so check yours. Our sick-day swim lesson decision guide walks through the common cases.

⛈️ What Happens If the Pool Cancels?

Facility closures, weather and maintenance can cancel a class through no fault of yours. Schools usually reschedule or add the class back, but how it works differs. Ask how you will be told, how far ahead, and whether you need to do anything to claim the class. Keep the date in your tracker either way, so a cancelled class does not quietly disappear from the session.

🏊 How Do You Protect Progress After a Missed Class?

Tell your instructor when you know a class will be missed and ask what to practice. Simple water-comfort games in the bath, such as blowing bubbles and practising back floats with a parent holding, help keep skills fresh. When your child returns, ask the instructor for a quick check-in on where they are. If several classes are missed in a row, our swimming progress tracker and fall skill retention checklist help you see what to work on. Consistency matters, which is why we also cover how often kids should take swim lessons.

📋 Simple Habits That Save Make-Ups

Put your class schedule in your family calendar, along with make-up deadlines. Message the school as early as you can. Book the make-up while you are still in touch, rather than leaving it until the end of the session. 🏊 Choosing a School With Clear Policies

Clear, written attendance and make-up policies are a good sign that a school is well run. When you compare programs, put the policy questions next to class size and instructor quality. Frequently Asked Questions

How do swim lesson make-ups usually work?

It depends entirely on the school. Some offer a make-up class within a set window, some give credits or tokens, and some offer nothing for a missed class. The safest approach is to ask for the policy in writing before you enroll and to note the deadline for booking any make-up.

Do I get a make-up if my child is sick?

Many schools do offer a make-up or credit for illness, but rules differ, and some ask for notice before class starts. Check your school’s policy. Whatever the policy, keep a child home if they have diarrhea, a fever or other signs of illness, so that the pool stays safe for everyone.

What if the pool cancels the class?

When the school or facility cancels a class, most schools reschedule it or add it back to the session, but the way this works varies. Ask what happens for facility closures and weather, and keep a note of the date in the tracker.

Do make-up lessons expire?

Often, yes. Many schools set a deadline, such as the end of the session or a fixed number of weeks. Because rules differ, write the expiry date next to each missed class as soon as it happens.

How many missed lessons is too many?

There is no single number, but skills fade when children are out of the water for long gaps. If your child misses several classes in a row, ask the instructor for a quick recap of where they are and what to practice before their next lesson.

Should I tell the instructor when my child will miss class?

Yes, when you can. A quick message helps the instructor plan groups and, at many schools, is required to qualify for a make-up. It also lets you ask what skills to practice while your child is away.

Can I use a missed class credit for a different day or level?

Sometimes, but not always. Some schools tie make-ups to the same level, or to specific days, or have limited spaces. Ask about the process for booking a make-up and whether it can be used at a different location.

Where do I find my swim school’s make-up policy?

Look in the enrollment agreement, the parent handbook or the FAQ on the school’s website, or ask the front desk. Our swim school policy fine print checklist lists the questions to ask about make-ups, cancellations and refunds.
