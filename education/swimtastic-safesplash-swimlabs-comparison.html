Swimtastic vs. SafeSplash vs. SwimLabs: What's Really the Difference?

When you search for swim schools in your area, you might find a Swimtastic, a SafeSplash, and a SwimLabs within a few miles of each other — and assume they're competing brands. They share the same corporate parent. Here's what that means for your family, and what actually differs between them.

Quick Answer: Swimtastic, SafeSplash, and SwimLabs are all owned by Youth Enrichment Brands (YEB), the same private equity-backed parent company. They share the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise guarantee and broadly similar curriculum frameworks. SwimLabs is the most differentiated — emphasizing video analysis technology, and unlike the other two it publishes 6 self-rescue skills (not 10) under its S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise. For parents of young beginners, the specific location's instructors and culture matter more than which brand name is on the sign.

🏢 Are Swimtastic, SafeSplash, and SwimLabs Owned by the Same Company?

Yes. Swimtastic, SafeSplash, and SwimLabs are all franchise brands of the same corporate parent — Youth Enrichment Brands, which acquired the swim-school franchisor Streamline Brands in 2022. They share ownership, corporate training, curriculum guidelines, and the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise guarantee. Most parents don't realize these aren't independent competitors.

The swim school franchise industry has undergone significant private equity-driven consolidation over the past decade. Streamline Brands — acquired in 2022 by Youth Enrichment Brands (YEB), and since rebranded to SafeSplash — assembled a portfolio of swim school brands including Swimtastic Swim School, SafeSplash Swim School, SwimLabs, and Saf-T-Swim — Long Island's dominant swim school, which keeps its own storefront name while running the shared SafeSplash curriculum, website platform, and parent app — plus AquaKids, The Swim Revolution, and Fish Tails, among others.

In 2025, the company further consolidated several of its brands under the SafeSplash umbrella in many markets. This consolidation means that in many cities, brands that appear to be separate competing businesses are actually franchises of the same corporate parent, sharing ownership, corporate support structures, training standards, and guarantee programs.

Most parents have no idea this consolidation happened. They see three different brand names and assume they're doing comparison shopping between genuinely independent businesses. This guide gives you the accurate picture so you can make an informed decision.

📊 Context: YEB's portfolio represents hundreds of swim school locations across the United States under multiple brand names. This makes it one of the largest swim school networks in the country by location count — comparable in scale to nationally recognized restaurant or fitness franchise networks. Understanding the franchise model helps parents interpret what "brand standards" actually mean at the local level.

🤝 What Do Swimtastic, SafeSplash, and SwimLabs Have in Common?

All three share corporate ownership, the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise guarantee, warm-water pools (88–92°F), small class sizes, perpetual enrollment, and the same target customer: families with children six months through school age.

All three brands operate under the same corporate ownership and share several significant elements. Most prominently: the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise guarantee program. This is the same program at all three brands — if a child doesn't master 10 specific self-rescue skills within 12 months of enrollment, the school provides up to two months of free additional lessons. The terms are identical across brands: weekly attendance required, maximum four absences, makeup lessons within two weeks of any absence.

Corporate training standards and franchise support systems are shared. Instructors at Swimtastic, SafeSplash, and SwimLabs locations receive onboarding and training through the same YEB corporate framework. Curriculum guidelines originate from the same parent organization. Franchise marketing and enrollment systems are shared infrastructure.

All three target the same primary customer: families with children ages six months through school age who are seeking progressive swim instruction in a dedicated swim school (vs. a YMCA or community pool). All three use warm-water pools (typically 88–92°F for young children), small class sizes as a selling point, and perpetual enrollment models where children advance when skills are mastered rather than on a fixed session calendar. This shared operating model is consistent with the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) position, which cites research finding that formal swim lessons reduce drowning risk by 88% for children ages 1–4 — a benefit that depends far more on lesson frequency and instructor quality than on which brand name is on the door.

🐬 What Is Swimtastic, and Which Ages Is It Built For?

Swimtastic is the portfolio's early-childhood-focused brand, serving ages six months through about 8. Its "Bubbles to Butterfly" curriculum runs eight levels (Beginner 1–4, Intermediate 1–4), with the T.A.P. therapeutic program and WAVE team-readiness bridge as Swimtastic-specific add-ons.

Swimtastic Swim School is primarily positioned as a children's swim school for ages six months and up, with particular emphasis on early childhood (infants through age 8). The "Bubbles to Butterfly" phrase used in Swimtastic marketing refers to their curriculum progression from basic water orientation through butterfly stroke technique.

Swimtastic's curriculum uses a level system (Beginner 1–4, Intermediate 1–4) with specific skill requirements at each level. The T.A.P. (Therapeutic Aquatic Program) for children with physical or developmental challenges is a Swimtastic-specific program offering that distinguishes it from a generic swim school experience.

The WAVE program — a team-readiness program for swimmers in the Intermediate 2+ range who are interested in competitive or recreational swim teams — is another Swimtastic-specific offering that provides a bridge between recreational lessons and swim team participation. This isn't a feature of every location; confirm local availability when researching specific franchises.

💦 What Is SafeSplash, and Why Were Other Locations Rebranded to It?

SafeSplash is the YEB portfolio's primary umbrella brand. In 2025, many Swimtastic and AquaKids locations were rebranded to SafeSplash, making it the dominant brand by location count. Curriculum is essentially identical to Swimtastic.

SafeSplash Swim School is the brand that YEB has used as its primary umbrella brand during recent consolidation efforts. In many markets, locations that operated as Swimtastic or AquaKids have been rebranded to SafeSplash. In 2025, YEB moved more locations under the SafeSplash banner, making it the dominant brand name in the portfolio by location count in many regions.

From a curriculum standpoint, SafeSplash and Swimtastic are highly similar. Both use the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise, warm water pools, and small class sizes. The "4 C's of Progress" framework — Comfortable, Capable, Confident, Competitive — is now the standardized curriculum across the entire portfolio (SafeSplash, SwimLabs, Swimtastic, Saf-T-Swim, Miller Swim School, and Sharkie’s Academy), replacing brand-specific ladders like Swimtastic's Bubbles to Butterfly as locations consolidate onto the shared SafeSplash system. We break down what each stage actually teaches in our 4 C's of Progress decoder.

If you're comparing a Swimtastic and a SafeSplash in the same area, the comparison most likely comes down to specific location quality, instructor experience, and which facility has better availability for your schedule — not meaningful curriculum differences.

🔬 What Makes SwimLabs Different?

SwimLabs is the most differentiated brand — it emphasizes underwater video analysis and in-pool mirrors for visual feedback on stroke technique, rather than the current-generating “flow pool” channels sometimes associated with the brand. SwimLabs’ own site lists Infant, Toddler and Beginner 1 lessons alongside advanced and competitive programs, though its technology-driven approach delivers the most value once a child is old enough to actively use visual correction (typically 5+, especially 8+).

SwimLabs is the most meaningfully differentiated brand of the three. Per SwimLabs’ own site, its locations use in-pool mirrors and underwater cameras for real-time and recorded video feedback — swimmers can watch their own stroke mechanics and correct technique with visual reference, rather than relying on a coach's verbal description alone.

SwimLabs also emphasizes underwater video analysis as a core instructional tool: swimmers can see their own stroke mechanics on video, allowing for visual feedback that accelerates technique correction. This is a legitimate and research-supported instructional approach — visual feedback does measurably improve motor skill acquisition at appropriate developmental ages (generally 7 and older).

SwimLabs’ own site lists Infant, Toddler and Beginner 1 lessons as part of its regular progression, alongside Children’s Beginner 2–4, Intermediate, and Advanced levels, plus programs for adults, competitive swimmers, and adaptive swimmers — so it is not restricted to older children. That said, its signature differentiator (video-feedback technology) delivers the most value once a child is old enough to actively use visual correction, generally 5 and up, particularly 8+. A family choosing purely on early-childhood water orientation will find any of the three brands’ warm-water pools and shared curriculum framework serve similarly; SwimLabs’ video technology is an added tool most useful at the stroke-refinement stage.

📋 How Does the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise Actually Work?

If a child doesn't master the required self-rescue skill list within 12 months — 10 skills at Swimtastic and SafeSplash, 6 at SwimLabs — the school provides up to two months of free additional lessons. Eligibility requires weekly attendance, no more than four absences, and makeup lessons within two weeks of any missed lesson — terms strict enough that some families won't qualify.

The S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise is the shared marketing guarantee across all three brands, and its terms deserve careful reading — starting with the skill count, which is not the same everywhere. The promise: if a child doesn't demonstrate mastery of the required self-rescue skill list within 12 months (10 skills at Swimtastic and SafeSplash; 6 at SwimLabs, which omits a controlled back float), the school provides up to two months of free additional lessons.

The conditions that must be met: the child must attend weekly (not less frequently), accumulate no more than four absences in the 12-month period, and complete any makeup lessons within two weeks of the original missed lesson. A 14-day pause (for illness, vacation, etc.) resets the 12-month clock in some interpretations — this is a key detail that families should clarify directly with their location before enrolling.

The guarantee provides up to two months of free lessons — not an indefinite guarantee of skill mastery, and not a refund. For a family that maintains perfect attendance and the child still doesn't achieve the applicable skill benchmark (10 at Swimtastic/SafeSplash, 6 at SwimLabs), two additional months of lessons is meaningful value. For a family with any absences or scheduling challenges, the eligibility conditions are strict enough that the guarantee may not apply. Independent of any brand guarantee, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) reports drowning is the #1 cause of unintentional death for children ages 1–4, and the self-rescue framework these brands teach — 10 skills at Swimtastic/SafeSplash, 6 at SwimLabs — directly targets that risk window. Ask your specific location exactly how the terms are administered locally.

🏪 How Does Franchise Ownership Affect the Quality of My Child's Lessons?

The local franchisee determines most of what parents actually experience — instructor hiring, training execution, and facility management all happen at the individual location. A well-run franchise of any of these brands will outperform a poorly-run franchise regardless of which name is on the sign.

All of these brands operate through individual franchisees — independent business owners who license the brand name, curriculum framework, and support systems from the corporate parent. This franchise structure has a critical implication: the quality of your experience is primarily determined by the local franchisee's management, the instructors they hire and train, and the culture they build at the specific location.

A well-managed Swimtastic franchise with excellent instructors will deliver a better experience than a poorly managed SafeSplash location regardless of which brand is "better" on paper. Conversely, a well-run SafeSplash or SwimLabs will outperform a mediocre independent swim school.

Before enrolling at any of these brands, take these steps: visit the specific location in person, observe a lesson if permitted, speak directly with the instructor who would teach your child, ask about instructor tenure and turnover, and read recent local reviews (not the national brand reviews). The brand name is a starting point for your research, not a guarantee of quality at a specific address. The American Red Cross Learn-to-Swim framework is a useful neutral reference point against which you can evaluate what any franchise claims to teach.

❓ What Should You Ask Before Enrolling at Any of These Brands?

Ask about instructor certification and CPR currency, actual class ratios (not advertised maximums), location-specific S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise administration, pool temperature monitoring, and which specific instructor will teach your child. These answers matter far more than the brand logo.

Ask about instructor certification: what credentials do instructors hold, how recently were CPR certifications renewed, and what is the school's ongoing instructor training process? Ask about class ratios: what is the actual student-to-instructor ratio in your child's specific class level, not the advertised maximum? Ask about the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise terms specific to their location: exactly how is attendance tracked, what constitutes an eligible makeup lesson, and how is the 12-month clock managed around pauses?

Ask about pool temperature: what is the standard operating temperature, and how is it monitored? For very young children, this is a non-negotiable quality indicator. Ask about the specific instructor who will teach your child: their experience level, their approach to children with water anxiety, and how they communicate progress to parents after each lesson.

These questions apply equally to all three brands and to any swim school you consider. The answers reveal far more about the experience your child will have than which corporate logo is on the sign.

What Do Swimtastic, SafeSplash and SwimLabs Cost?

None of the three publishes national pricing, because all are franchised and each location sets its own rates. Prices vary by market, class type, and group size, so the only reliable number is the one your nearest location quotes. When you call, ask for the monthly rate, the registration or enrollment fee, whether an annual membership renews, and what notice period is required to stop billing.

This is not evasiveness on the brands' part — it is how franchising works. A franchisee carries their own lease, payroll, and pool heating costs, so a price set nationally would be wrong almost everywhere. It does mean that any figure you find quoted online for these brands is one family's local rate rather than a national price.

How to compare quotes fairly

Ask each location the same four questions so the quotes are comparable: what the total first-month cost is including every fee, what the maximum class size is at your child's level, how make-up lessons work if you miss, and how a child moves up a level. Franchise brands share a curriculum, but class size, pool temperature, and instructor tenure are set locally — and those are what your child actually experiences.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Swimtastic, SafeSplash, and SwimLabs the same company?

Yes — all three brands are owned by Youth Enrichment Brands (YEB), which acquired the swim-school franchisor Streamline Brands in 2022 — the same private equity-backed parent company. They share corporate ownership, the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise guarantee program, and similar franchise operating standards. Individual locations are independently owned franchises, which means your experience may vary by location regardless of which brand you choose.

What is the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise and does it apply to all three brands?

The S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise is a shared guarantee program across Swimtastic, SafeSplash, and SwimLabs, but the skill count is not identical: Swimtastic and SafeSplash require mastering 10 specific self-rescue skills (including a controlled back float) within 12 months, while SwimLabs requires 6 (SwimLabs' list omits the back-float skill). If a child doesn't meet the applicable count, the school provides up to two months of free additional lessons. Participation requires weekly attendance, no more than 4 absences, and makeup lessons completed within two weeks of absence.

What is the real difference between Swimtastic and SafeSplash?

Swimtastic and SafeSplash use essentially the same curriculum framework and guarantee program under the same corporate ownership. The primary differences are geographic market (you'll find one or the other depending on your location, not both), some facility design differences, and branding. From a curriculum and quality perspective, the brands are largely equivalent.

How is SwimLabs different from Swimtastic and SafeSplash?

SwimLabs is the most differentiated brand of the three. Rather than the current-generating “flow pool” channels sometimes associated with the brand, SwimLabs’ own site describes standard pools with in-pool mirrors and underwater video analysis technology that give swimmers visual feedback on technique. SwimLabs lists Infant, Toddler and Beginner 1 lessons alongside advanced and competitive programs, though its technology-driven differentiation is most pronounced for children refining stroke technique. All three share corporate ownership and the S.A.F.E.R. Swimmer Promise.

Does it matter which brand I choose if they're all owned by the same company?

For most parents of young children just beginning lessons, the brand distinction matters less than the specific location's quality: the instructors' training and experience, the pool temperature and facilities, the instructor-to-student ratio, and whether the location's culture is a good fit for your child. Visit the specific location and speak with instructors before committing, regardless of brand.
