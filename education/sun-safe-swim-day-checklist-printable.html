Sun-Safe Swim Day Checklist

Water reflects UV and kids overheat faster than adults, so a great swim day needs a sun plan too. Pack the shade, time the sunscreen, push the water, and know the warning signs before anyone gets too much sun.

🧴 Pack for the Sun (before you leave)

  • Broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF 30 or higher — enough for the whole family, all day.
  • Sun-protective clothing: rash guards or UPF swim shirts, wide-brim hats, and sunglasses.
  • Shade you can count on — a pop-up tent or umbrella, since pool and beach shade runs out fast.
  • Plenty of water and a cooler — more than you think you'll need.
  • Water shoes or sandals — hot decks, sand, and metal ladders burn bare feet.
  • Check the UV index and forecast; plan the longest swim around the cooler hours if you can.

🧴 Sunscreen, Done Right

  • Apply 15 minutes before heading outside, onto dry skin.
  • Use enough — about a shot-glass amount for the body, and don't miss ears, feet, neck, and the part in the hair.
  • Reapply every 2 hours — and right after swimming or toweling off, even if it says "water-resistant."
  • Babies under 6 months: keep them out of direct sun with shade and clothing rather than relying on sunscreen.
  • Sunscreen buys protection, not extra time — still seek shade, especially from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. when UV is strongest.

💧 Beat the Heat & Stay Hydrated

  • Offer water every 20–30 minutes — kids overheat faster than adults and often forget to drink.
  • Being in the water doesn't stop dehydration — sun and activity still pull fluids out.
  • Take shade and cool-down breaks out of the sun on a schedule, not just when someone complains.
  • Never leave a child in a parked car, even for a minute — interior heat rises dangerously fast.
  • Dress light between swims and skip heavy, dark cover-ups in peak heat.

🚑 Know the Warning Signs

  • Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, pale or clammy skin, headache, dizziness, nausea, or crankiness. Move to shade, sip water, cool the skin, and rest.
  • Heat stroke is an emergency — call 911: hot, red skin, a high temperature, confusion, fainting, or a child who has stopped sweating. Cool them aggressively while help is on the way.
  • Sunburn: get out of the sun, cool the skin, and hydrate — blistering or a burn on a baby needs a doctor.
  • When in doubt, cool down and hydrate first — then decide if you need more help.
