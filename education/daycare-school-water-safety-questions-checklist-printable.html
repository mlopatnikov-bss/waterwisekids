Daycare & School Water Safety Questions

Most of your child's water exposure now happens where you can't see it — water tables, sprinkler days, and pool field trips. Take these 20 questions to enrollment night, a tour, or a parent-teacher conference. Check off each one as you get a clear, specific answer.

💧 What Water Is On the Property?

  • What water is on site? Water tables, buckets, sinks, toilets, wading pools, sprinklers, a swimming pool.
  • Is there open water on or near the grounds? A retention pond, creek, drainage ditch, or fountain — and is it fenced?
  • How are water tables and buckets emptied and stored immediately after use, so nothing sits filled and unattended?
  • Can young children reach bathrooms unaccompanied? Toilets and sinks are real hazards for toddlers.
  • If there's a pool, what barriers exist? A four-sided fence, self-closing and self-latching gate, and door alarms.

👁️ Supervision & Ratios

  • Who is assigned to watch children during water play — by name or role, not "someone"?
  • Does that person have any other duty at the same time? The right answer is no.
  • What is the adult-to-child ratio during water activities specifically? Water play should tighten ratios, not match classroom ratios.
  • How are phones handled during water activities? Watching the water means eyes up, screens away.
  • How are children counted, and how often? A headcount at fixed intervals is the standard.

🚌 Before You Sign the Field Trip Slip

  • Where exactly is the group going — a lifeguarded pool, a water park, or open water like a lake or beach?
  • How many adults are supervising, and how many watch the water only? Chaperoning and water-watching are different jobs.
  • Will lifeguards be on duty, and how many? Lifeguards supplement your group's supervision — they don't replace it.
  • Are children swim-tested or wristbanded by ability before they enter the water?
  • Are Coast Guard-approved life jackets provided for non-swimmers? Never floaties or puddle jumpers as a substitute.

🚨 Training & Emergency Response

  • Which staff hold current CPR and first-aid certification, and when was it last renewed?
  • How many certified staff are on site at any given time, including early drop-off and aftercare?
  • Is there a written plan if a child is found in water? If it isn't written down, it isn't a plan.
  • Do you have a plan for a child who wanders or elopes? Critical if your child has any history of wandering.
  • How and how quickly are parents notified of any water-related incident or near-miss?

🚩 Answers That Should Worry You

  • "Someone's always watching" — no one person owns the job.
  • No written emergency water plan, or uncertainty about CPR certification.
  • Water activities staffed at the same ratio as classroom time.
  • A field trip slip with no detail on location, lifeguards, or life jackets.
  • Defensiveness when you ask. Quality programs welcome these questions.
