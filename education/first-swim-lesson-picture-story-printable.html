My First Swim Lesson: A Picture Story

A fill-in story to read with your child before their first swim lesson. Make it about the real pool, read it together for a few days before and again just before you go, keep it honest, and talk about what really happened afterwards.

📖 The Story

1This is me
Photo or drawing

My name is __________. I am going to swim lessons at __________ on __________.

2Going to the pool
Photo or drawing

We go to the pool building. Inside it may be warm and a little loud. Voices and splashes can echo. That is OK.

3Getting ready
Photo or drawing

I put on my swimsuit. I use the bathroom before class. My grown-up keeps my towel and bag.

4Meeting my teacher
Photo or drawing

My teacher’s name is __________. My teacher helps kids learn to be safe in the water.

5Waiting for my turn
Photo or drawing

I sit on the side and listen. I get in the water only when my teacher says it is my turn.

6Feeling the water
Photo or drawing

The water may feel cool at first. My body gets used to it. I can hold on to the wall or my teacher.

7Learning new things
Photo or drawing

My teacher shows me what to do. I might kick, blow bubbles or float on my back. Sometimes water splashes my face. I can wipe my face and try again.

8If I feel nervous
Photo or drawing

It is OK to feel nervous. I can take a big breath and tell my teacher. My grown-up is watching from __________.

9All done
Photo or drawing

When class is over, I say goodbye to my teacher. I get my towel, change and go home. Next __________ I go again.

10My pool rule
Photo or drawing

I only go in or near water when a grown-up says yes and is watching. At lessons, at home and everywhere.

My teacher’s name was __________. The easy part was __________. The hard part was __________. When I felt nervous, I __________. Draw it here:

🛋️ How to Read It Together

  • Start early. Begin a few days to a week before the first lesson, at a calm time.
  • Read daily, then just before. Once a day in the run-up and once more before you leave for the pool.
  • Let your child lead. They hold the pages, point at the pictures and ask questions. Answer honestly.
  • Fix what doesn’t fit. Cross out lines that don’t match your class and add ones that do, such as goggles or a parent-and-me class.
  • No promises. Skip “it will be fun” or “you won’t get wet”. True and calm works better.
  • Fade it out. Once the routine feels familiar, read it less often.
Needs more support? For autistic children or kids who need more support, ask their therapist, speech pathologist or teacher to adapt the story. Children with limited comprehension or who are nonverbal may do better with a photo schedule or first/then board. Tell the swim school about your child’s needs before the first class.
Remember: swim lessons are one layer of protection and do not make children drown-proof. Keep close, constant adult supervision, barriers around pools and a named Water Watcher whenever children are in or near water.
