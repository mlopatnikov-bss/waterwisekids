Swimmer's Ear Prevention Checklist

Swimmer's ear (otitis externa) is an outer-ear-canal infection caused by water trapped in the ear — and it's very preventable. Use this one-page checklist to drain, dry, and protect your child's ears after every swim, so a sore ear never sidelines them from lessons.

✅ After Every Swim or Bath: Drain & Dry

  • Tilt the head to each side and gently tug the earlobe to let trapped water drain out of the canal.
  • Dry the outer ear with a soft towel — never jam the towel or a finger into the canal.
  • Use a hair dryer on the lowest, coolest setting, held about a foot away, to evaporate leftover moisture in prone swimmers.
  • Make it the same routine every time — pool, lake, or bath — so it becomes automatic.

🚫 Never Do This

  • No cotton swabs, fingers, or hairpins in the ear canal — they scratch the skin and invite infection.
  • Don't remove earwax — it's the ear's natural defense; dry the canal, don't clean inside it.
  • Don't ignore an itchy or achy ear — early treatment keeps swimmer's ear minor.

🏊 Extra Protection for Frequent Swimmers

  • Well-fitting swim earplugs or an ear-covering swim cap keep water out of the canal.
  • Ask your pediatrician about drying drops — only for kids without ear tubes or a perforated eardrum.
  • Choose well-maintained pools with proper chlorine and pH; pack earplugs and a towel in the swim bag.

🚨 Stop Swimming & See a Doctor If You Notice

  • Ear pain — especially pain that worsens when you tug the earlobe or press the ear.
  • Drainage from the ear (clear or pus-like) or a plugged, muffled-hearing feeling.
  • Call promptly for fever, severe pain, or swelling/redness spreading around the ear, face, or neck.
