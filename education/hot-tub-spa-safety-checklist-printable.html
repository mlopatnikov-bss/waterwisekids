Hot Tub & Spa Safety Checklist for Kids

A hot tub is not a warm little pool — set the limits before anyone gets in, every time

🌡️ Age, Time & Temperature Limits

  • Under age 5: keep them out — pediatric guidance says young children should not use hot tubs at all. Their bodies cannot handle the heat and the entrapment risk is highest.
  • Ages 5–12: about 5 minutes, max — keep sessions short and end them before any child feels warm or tired.
  • Turn the temperature DOWN — set it to roughly 98°F or lower for children, well below the adult maximum of 104°F.
  • Heads and shoulders above the water — children should not fully submerge in a spa.
  • Out at the first warning sign — flushed skin, dizziness, headache, nausea, or "I feel funny" means out of the water, cool down, and hydrate.

🌀 Prevent Drain Entrapment

  • Drain cover is intact and CPSC-compliant — never run the spa with a cracked, loose, or missing cover. Suction can trap hair or a limb and hold a child under.
  • Long hair tied up — a bun or braid before entering. Loose hair can wrap around a drain in an instant.
  • No loose clothing or dangling accessories — anything that can be pulled into a drain comes off first.
  • Kids stay away from drains and jets — never sit on or play near a suction outlet.
  • Know the emergency pump shutoff — if someone is entrapped, cut the pump power FIRST; do not pull forcefully. Find the switch/breaker before turning the tub on.

👀 Supervision & Keeping Kids Out When Closed

  • One adult within arm's reach — touch supervision. Bubbles, steam, and noise hide a struggling child, so watch even closer than at a pool.
  • Water Watcher has no other job — no phone, no grill, no conversation. Watching the water is the whole task.
  • Never leave a child alone in or near the spa — not even for a moment to "grab something."
  • Locking, rigid cover on when not in use — a soft or unlatched cover is not a barrier. A curious child can slip under it.
  • Spa area fenced or gated — keep children from reaching the tub between soaks.

🏨 Hotel & Vacation-Rental Spas

  • Check the drain cover — visible and intact, or skip it. You don't know the maintenance history.
  • Water is clear, not cloudy — murky water signals poor upkeep.
  • Temperature display works and reads no higher than needed — if you can't lower it, keep kids out.
  • When in doubt, use the regular pool instead — an unknown spa is not worth the risk.

🆘 Emergency Readiness

  • At least one adult knows infant/child CPR — the skill that matters most in the minutes before help arrives.
  • Phone at the spa for 911 — if a child is overheated or in distress, shout for help, call 911, cut the pump, and start rescue immediately.
  • Home address posted or memorized — anyone calling 911 must give the full address instantly.
  • Know heat-emergency signs — confusion, fainting, or stopping sweating in the heat is a medical emergency. Call 911.

Critical Reminders for Every Spa

  • Children overheat in minutes — a spa that feels pleasant to an adult can put a child's body under serious heat stress fast.
  • Drain suction can hold a child underwater — intact covers, tied-up hair, and a known shutoff prevent it.
  • Drowning is fast and silent — bubbles and noise make it even harder to notice. Keep eyes on the water.
  • Swim lessons lower risk — they don't replace the rules — lessons cut drowning risk for ages 1–4 by up to 88% (AAP), but age, time, and temperature limits still apply, every time.
