Water Safety Activities You Can Do at Home

No pool required. Almost all of drowning prevention is dry-land work — spotting hazards, knowing never to go in after someone, checking a life jacket, calling for help, and staying calm. Run one of these six activities every couple of weeks and your child keeps the habits all year.

🎯 Six No-Pool Activities

Each one takes about five to ten minutes. Tick the box each time you run it — repetition is the whole point.

  • 🏠 1. The Room-by-Room Hazard Hunt
    Walk the house together and let your child point out every place water collects — bathtub, toilet, buckets, mop pails, pet bowls, coolers, rain barrels, hot tub. Talk about which ones an adult empties after use. Repeat it any time you move or arrive somewhere new.
  • 🦺 2. The Life Jacket Fit Challenge
    Put the life jacket on in the living room, fasten every buckle, then lift straight up by the shoulder straps. If the ears and chin slip through, it’s too big. Do this before every trip — kids grow out of jackets between seasons.
  • 🪢 3. Reach, Throw, Don’t Go — on the Carpet
    Lay a rope or pool noodle on the floor. Your child lies down and extends it instead of stepping forward, then practices throwing a cushion to a target. Every rep starts the same way: yell for an adult first. Never swim out to someone.
  • 👀 4. The Water Watcher Handoff
    Rehearse the handoff out loud: one adult wears the badge, watches only the water, and says the words “you have the watch” before handing it to the next adult. Practice it at the dinner table so it’s automatic poolside.
  • ☎️ 5. The Call-for-Help Drill
    Two steps, in order: shout for the nearest grown-up, then call 911. Have your child practice saying your street address out loud. Time it — kids love beating their own record, and the address is the part adults forget.
  • 🌟 6. Dry-Land Float & Breath Practice
    On a bed or rug: chin up, ears in the “water,” belly up, arms and legs wide like a starfish, slow breathing. Then practice blowing bubbles through a straw into a cup. Both rehearse the calm breathing that a real float depends on.

🔁 Make It Stick

  • Short and frequent beats long and rare. Ten minutes every couple of weeks builds habits. One long talk once a year does not.
  • Attach it to something already in your week. Check the jacket before a trip. Run the hazard hunt when you travel. Rehearse the watcher handoff before a family gathering.
  • Keep it calm, never scary. You’re building respect for water, not fear of it. Fear makes lessons harder later.
  • Let your child teach it back. Have them run one activity for a sibling or grandparent. Teaching it is how it sticks.
  • Do it in the off-season. The months with no pool around are the best time to practice — and the easiest time to lose ground.

⚠️ Important Reminder

  • These activities build knowledge and habits — they do not build the physical skill of getting a breath and getting to safety. Only in-water practice with a qualified instructor does that. No child is ever “drown-proof,” and every swimmer still needs constant supervision, barriers, and properly fitted life jackets.
