Swim Lesson Skin, Hair & Eye Care Checklist

Weekly swim lessons mean weekly pool water on skin, hair and eyes. A two-minute routine before and after class keeps most kids comfortable, and comfortable kids keep swimming. Use this page to set the routine, spot problems early and know when to ask a doctor.

🎒 Step 1: Before Class

  • Quick rinse or shower. A short shower before getting in helps wash off sweat, lotion and dirt that use up pool chemicals and can irritate eyes and skin.
  • Bathroom stop. Pee and sweat react with pool chlorine, and that reaction is a main cause of red, stinging eyes. A bathroom trip before class helps.
  • Tie back long hair. A braid, bun or swim cap keeps hair out of eyes and mouth and means less hair to rinse.
  • Goggles packed and fitted. Well-fitting goggles are the simplest protection for stinging eyes. Check the strap and the seal on dry land.
  • Sensitive skin plan. If your child has dry or eczema-prone skin, ask your pediatrician what to use beforehand, and tell the instructor.

🚿 Step 2: Right After Class

  • Rinse the whole body. A warm, short shower with mild soap removes pool water from skin and hair. Use the on-site showers if there are any.
  • Wash hair. Use a gentle shampoo, and a conditioner for long or curly hair. Comb out gently while it is wet.
  • Pat dry, do not rub. Pay attention to skin folds, behind the knees and elbows, and between toes.
  • Moisturize within a few minutes. A fragrance-free moisturizer on damp skin helps replace moisture lost in the pool.
  • Do not rub eyes. If eyes sting, rinse with clean water and let them settle. Rubbing makes irritation worse.
  • Dry ears gently. Tilt the head to each side and dry the outer ear with a towel. See our swimmer’s ear checklist for more.
  • Rinse the swimsuit. Rinse in fresh water after every class so chemicals do not sit against the skin next time.

🔍 Step 3: What We Are Watching For

Call your pediatrician if skin is cracked, weeping, blistered or spreading, if your child has eye pain, swelling or changes in vision, or if any breathing symptom keeps coming back. This checklist is for everyday comfort, not for diagnosis.
