Ice & Frozen Pond Safety Checklist

A frozen pond is never a safe playground by default. No natural ice is guaranteed safe, and kids cannot judge it. Use this page to set the rules, plan outings and know exactly what to do in an emergency.

🧒 Step 1: Rules for Kids

  • Ice is off limits unless a grown-up says it is safe. Say the rule out loud together before the first freeze.
  • Never go out for a ball, a pet or a friend. Tell an adult right away and call for help instead.
  • Stay away from edges, docks, inlets and moving water. These spots are often the thinnest, even when the ice looks solid.
  • Never go on the ice alone. Always have an adult watching, and tell them where you are going.
  • Know the words: reach or throw, don’t go. Kids can help by calling 911 and getting an adult, never by walking out.
  • Stay off ice that looks dark, slushy, cracked or has open water. When in doubt, stay out and tell an adult.

🧤 Step 2: Adult Pre-Outing Check

  • Pick a monitored rink or posted conditions if you can. Natural ice is never guaranteed safe, however it looks.
  • Check for moving water. Inlets, outlets, docks, springs and shorelines can hide thin ice.
  • Assign one adult on active watch. Phone in hand, no distractions, eyes on the children the whole time.
  • Bring a reach pole or rope and something that floats. Keep them within arm’s reach, not in the car.
  • Dress for cold and check life jacket fit. Warm layers, hats and mittens, plus a snug flotation device for younger children near the ice.
  • Set the exit plan. Agree where to meet, who calls 911 and what the signal is to leave the ice.
  • Skip it if conditions are unclear. A cancelled outing is always better than a rescue.

General guidance is at least 4 inches of clear, solid ice for one person on foot, but thickness varies across a pond and appearance is not reliable. Treat any unconfirmed ice as unsafe.

🛟 Step 4: If Someone Falls Through

  • Call 911 first. Give the exact location and say someone has fallen through ice.
  • Reach or throw. Do not go. Use a pole, branch, rope or floating object from solid ground, and lie flat if you must get closer.
  • Coach them. Call out to kick, keep the head up and stay calm until help arrives.
  • After rescue: warm up. Remove wet clothing, wrap in dry layers, and get medical care even if they seem fine.
Remember: “Reach or throw, don’t go.” Rescuers who walk onto the ice often become victims too.
