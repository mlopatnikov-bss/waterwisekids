Multi-Child Swim Lesson Cost Worksheet

Swim schools quote a weekly or per-lesson price for one child. Families enrolling two or three children pay something quite different once sibling discounts, per-child registration fees and annual fees are stacked up. ☎️ Ask Every School These 8 Questions First

  • “Do you offer a sibling or family discount?” — ask plainly. Many schools have one and never advertise it on the pricing page.
  • “Is it a percentage or a flat dollar amount, and how much?” — write the actual number, not “yes.”
  • “Which child does the discount apply to — the higher-priced or lower-priced enrollment?” — this can change the savings meaningfully when siblings are in different levels or lesson lengths.
  • “Does it apply to the third child too, or does it step down?” — some schools give 15% for child two and 10% for child three.
  • “Is there a registration fee, and is it per child or per family?” — a per-family fee is worth real money to a three-child household.
  • “Is there an annual or membership fee, and when does it renew?” — ask the renewal date, not just the amount.
  • “Do the children have to be enrolled at the same time, on the same day, or in the same session?” — scheduling conditions are the most common reason a promised discount never appears.
  • “If one child pauses or drops, what happens to the discount?” — ask before you enroll, not after.

🔎 Five Things Families Forget to Ask

  • Can the sibling discount be combined with anything else? Referral credits, prepay-the-year savings and military or first-responder rates sometimes stack — and sometimes explicitly do not.
  • Does the discount survive a level change? If child two moves into a longer or pricier class, confirm the family rate follows.
  • Is there a family cap? A few programs cap total household tuition regardless of how many children enroll. It is worth asking outright.
  • What is the pause or withdrawal policy? Multi-child households hit scheduling conflicts more often, so a flexible pause is worth real money.
  • Are back-to-back sibling class times available? Two lessons an hour apart on the same day is a hidden cost in fuel, childcare and evenings. Ask before you enroll.
  • Is financial assistance available? Many YMCAs and community programs offer sliding-scale or scholarship rates that beat any sibling discount.

Pricing Red Flags

  • The staff cannot tell you the total first-month charge — if nobody can add it up on the phone, you will find out on the card statement.
  • Fees appear only in the enrollment agreement — ask for every fee before you are asked for a card.
  • A long prepaid contract is required before you can watch a class — confident programs let the teaching sell itself.
  • “We'll work something out” instead of a number — get the family rate in writing on the enrollment form.
  • Price is the only thing that impressed you — ratios, instructor training and whether survival skills are taught early matter more than a 15% discount.
