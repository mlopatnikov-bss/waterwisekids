🖨️ Your Free Pool Safety Rules Poster Is Ready

Print this poster, laminate it, and post it at your pool. Works great for backyard pools, pool parties, and handing to babysitters.

← Back to Full Article
Quick Answer: The most important pool safety rules are to never swim alone, keep one undistracted adult assigned as the Water Watcher at all times, and require children to ask permission before entering the water. Drowning is the leading cause of unintentional death for U.S. children ages 1–4, and constant adult supervision is the single most effective way to prevent it.

According to the CDC, about 970 children die from drowning each year in the U.S., and a four-sided isolation pool fence reduces a child's drowning risk by 83%. The American Red Cross recommends rotating the Water Watcher every 15–20 minutes. The poster below is the short version; our full guide to the 10 backyard pool safety rules and how to enforce them by age explains what each rule actually prevents and which ones families most often drop.

10 Pool Safety Rules
Post, review, and enforce before every swim
📋

Review these rules with ALL swimmers and guests before entering the water. Consistency saves lives.

1

Never Swim Alone

Always use the buddy system. No exceptions — regardless of swimming ability.

2

An Adult Watches the Water

One adult is the designated Water Watcher — no phone, no food, just watching.

3

Ask Permission to Enter

Children must ask an adult before getting in. No exceptions.

4

No Running or Horseplay

Wet decks are slippery. Walk calmly. No diving in non-designated areas.

5

Respect "Stop" — Always

If anyone says stop, stop immediately. No dunking, no holding others underwater.

6

No Glass at the Pool

No glass containers of any kind. Plastic cups and bottles only.

7

Know Depth — Stay in Your Zone

Weaker swimmers stay in shallow water only. Know where deep end begins.

8

Tell an Adult When You Exit

Always tell an adult before leaving the pool or pool area.

9

Respect Safety Equipment

Don't move or play with life rings, throw bags, or rescue equipment.

10

Get Help Immediately

If someone is in trouble — shout, throw a flotation device, call 911. Act fast.

🆘

If You Suspect Drowning

Shout for the Water Watcher · Throw something that floats · Call 911 immediately · Do NOT enter the water unless trained · Begin CPR if swimmer is unresponsive and not breathing

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most important pool safety rules for kids?

The core rules are: never swim alone (always use the buddy system), assign one adult as the Water Watcher with no phone or distractions, require children to ask permission before entering, and ban running and horseplay on wet decks. These rules apply to backyard pools, pool parties, and any time kids are near water.

What is a Water Watcher and why does it matter?

A Water Watcher is one adult whose only job is to watch the water — no phone, no reading, no conversations. Because drowning is silent and can happen in under a minute, the American Red Cross recommends rotating the Water Watcher role every 15–20 minutes to stay alert.

How can parents prevent drowning at a backyard pool?

Use multiple layers of protection: a four-sided isolation fence with a self-latching gate (which the CDC reports reduces drowning risk by 83%), constant adult supervision, swim lessons, and CPR training. No single layer is enough on its own.

At what age should children learn pool safety rules?

Children can begin learning simple pool rules as toddlers. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends formal swim lessons starting around age 1, and water-safety habits should be reinforced every season.
