Free Trial Swim Lesson Scorecard

Trying a trial class? Rate what you see from 0 to 2, tick any red flags, ask your questions and compare schools. This page keeps your eyes on safety, teaching and your child’s comfort so you can decide calmly.

WaterWiseKids.com — Free water safety education for families

📝 Step 1: The Basics

Child’s name and age
School and location
Date and time of the trial
Instructor’s name
Class length and number of children in the group
What the trial includes (class, tour, assessment)
How to score. Give each item 2 for clearly yes, 1 for partly or unsure and 0 for no. Write a short note for anything you score 0 or 1.

🎒 Step 2: Before You Go

  • Ask what the trial includes and how long it lasts. Write it in Step 1.
  • Ask whether parents can watch. Follow the school’s rule on where to sit.
  • Tell the school about worries or medical needs. Fear of water, ear tubes, allergies or anything the instructor should know.
  • Pack swim gear, a towel and a warm layer. Use the swim lesson day checklist.
  • Tell your child what will happen. Keep it short, calm and positive.

🏊 Step 4: Teaching

Skills are taught in small, clear steps  
Every child gets turns in the water with little waiting  
The instructor adjusts for a nervous child and never forces  
You hear what your child worked on and what comes next  

💙 Step 5: My Child’s Comfort

CheckScore 0–2Notes
My child was interested at some point  
My child became more comfortable as class went on  
My child says they are willing to go back  
Remember. A child who cries or clings has not failed the trial. Note how the instructor responded.

🚩 Step 6: Red Flags — Stop and Ask

  • The instructor was on a phone or not watching the group. Ask who is responsible for watching the water.
  • Children waited unsupervised at the edge. Ask how the school supervises waiting children.
  • A child was pushed, dunked or held under against their will. Ask about the school’s teaching approach.
  • No clear answer about who is responsible for safety. Ask again, in writing if you can.
  • Pressure to sign up and pay on the spot. Take the paperwork home and decide later.
Any ticked box deserves a question before you enroll. If the answer does not settle your concern, try another school or watch another class.

❓ Step 7: Questions to Ask the School

  • How are children placed in levels? Ask what your child would start in.
  • What does progress look like? Ask how and when you will hear about it.
  • What is the total cost? Ask about registration, equipment and any other fees.
  • How do make-up classes and missed weeks work? Ask for the policy in writing.
  • What is the class size and who teaches? Ask whether the instructor stays the same.
  • What happens if my child is not ready? Ask about re-placing or pausing.

A lesson is one layer, not the only one. Close, undistracted supervision, a designated water watcher, barriers around pools and a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket near open water all matter. Lessons do not make a child drown-proof, at any age.
