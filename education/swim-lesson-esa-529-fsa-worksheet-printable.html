ESA, 529 and FSA Swim Lesson Worksheet

For families who want to pay for swim lessons with an education savings account, 529 plan, FSA or HSA. Ordinary lessons are not automatically eligible. Ask the administrator first, get the answer in writing and keep the records.

📖 Step 2: Which Rule Set Applies?

AccountWhat primary sources sayOur next step
Health FSA or HSAIRS FAQ Q11: swimming lessons for general health are not reimbursable, even if a doctor recommends them (see also IRS Pub. 502). 
529 planNo federal rule found that makes an ordinary child’s lesson a qualified expense (IRS Pub. 970). Nonqualified distributions can have tax consequences. 
State ESA or scholarshipMay allow some expenses under a named category. The administrator decides; a category is not approval. 
Dependent Care FSAFollows childcare rules. A stand-alone lesson and a day-camp program are different products. 
ABLE or otherSeparate rule set. Use the current program document. 

☎️ Step 3: Questions for the Administrator

  • Category. Which program and spending category would apply to swim lessons?
  • Provider and class. Does this named swim provider, and this specific class, qualify?
  • Pre-approval. Can I get written pre-approval before I pay? How do I request it?
  • Documents. Which invoice details, credentials or curriculum evidence do you need?
  • Payment route. Do you pay the provider directly, or do I pay and request reimbursement?
  • Record keeping. How long must I keep the records?

✍️ Step 4: The Written Answer

Decision: ☐ Approved ☐ Not eligible ☐ Need more information
How received: ☐ Email ☐ Portal ☐ Letter ☐ Phone only
Handbook or plan section cited
Conditions · Maximum amount · Date range
Phone only or “submit a claim and see”? Treat the expense as unapproved until you have the answer in writing.

🗂️ Step 5: Records to Keep

  • The written decision and the handbook or plan section it relies on.
  • An itemized invoice with the provider’s business name, dates of service, class description and rate.
  • Proof of payment such as a receipt or statement line.
  • Credential or curriculum evidence only if the administrator asked for it.
  • Schedule, make-up and refund records, since a credited or refunded lesson can change what you claim.

🏊 Step 6: Questions for the Swim School

  • Itemized invoices. Can you list dates, class name and rate on each invoice?
  • Third-party payment. How do you handle payment from a state program or reimbursement account?
  • Provider registration. Are you already a registered provider with our program, if it requires one?
  • Missed classes and withdrawal. What happens to prepaid sessions if we miss classes or withdraw?
Stop and check first if: a provider says “FSA eligible” or “ESA approved” without naming the program and showing the decision; anyone suggests calling an ordinary lesson therapy; or you are asked to prepay a large package before you have an answer. A plan-year deadline is never a reason to submit an expense that does not qualify.

💡 Step 7: If the Answer Is No

  • Ask about assistance. Ask local providers about financial assistance, sliding scales and free water safety sessions.
  • Apply to more than one program. Track each scholarship application and its follow-up date.
  • Compare pricing. Check sibling, multi-session and annual-fee costs before you enroll.
Remember: swim lessons are one layer of protection. They never replace close adult supervision, barriers around water and a named Water Watcher.
