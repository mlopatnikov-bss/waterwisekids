Free Trial Swim Lesson Scorecard: How to Rate a Trial Class Before You Enroll (Free Printable)

A free trial class is the best look you will get at a swim school before you pay, but it passes quickly and a nervous child can distract you from what matters. A simple scorecard keeps your eyes on safety, teaching and your child’s comfort, so you can decide calmly. Here is what to check and how to rate it, with a free one-page printable.

Quick Answer: During a trial lesson, rate three things from 0 to 2: safety and supervision, teaching, and your child’s comfort. Tick any red flag, such as an instructor not watching the water or a child being forced to put their face in. Then ask about levels, costs and make-up classes before you pay. 🔍 Why Treat a Trial Lesson Like an Interview?

A trial lesson works both ways. Your child is trying out the class, and you are trying out the school. It is easy to leave with only a feeling, such as “it seemed fine” or “she cried, so maybe not.” A scorecard turns that feeling into notes you can compare. Our free trial swim lessons guide explains what to ask and why, and this page gives you a form to fill in while you watch.

🎒 Before You Go

Ask the school what the trial includes, how long it lasts, whether parents can watch and what your child should wear and bring. Pack the basics from our swim lesson day checklist, and use the first swim lesson checklist to prepare your child. Tell your child what will happen in simple words, and tell the school about any worries, such as fear of water or a medical need. If you are comparing several programs, our swim school comparison worksheet and pool tour checklist pair well with the scorecard.

🛟 Rate Safety and Supervision First

Safety comes before teaching style. Look at who is watching the water: the instructor should be able to see every child in the group, and other staff should be watching the pool area. Children should know where to wait and when they may enter, with no one left alone at the edge. Check that doors and gates to the pool area are secured and that the deck is tidy. Score each item 2 for clearly yes, 1 for partly or unsure and 0 for no. For the pool itself, the pool tour checklist goes deeper on temperature, air quality and changing rooms.

🏊 Rate the Teaching

Good teaching is easy to see once you know what to look for. The instructor greets your child by name, explains what will happen and breaks skills into small steps. Every child gets turns in the water without long waits. The instructor adjusts when a child is nervous, praises effort and never pushes or holds a child under water. At the end, you should hear what your child worked on and what comes next. Our swim lesson quality checklist and swim instructor questions checklist offer more to look for and ask.

💙 Rate Your Child’s Comfort

Your child’s reaction counts, but one day is a small sample. Note whether your child was interested at some point, whether they became more comfortable as class went on and whether they are willing to go back. A child who cries or clings is not a failed trial, and what matters is how the instructor responds, with patience, small steps and choices instead of pressure. If fear is the main issue, see our overcoming water fear checklist and separation anxiety plan, and consider whether your child is ready using the swim lesson readiness checklist.

🚩 Red Flags That Mean Stop and Ask

Some things should not be averaged away by a good score elsewhere. Stop and ask the school about it if you see an instructor on a phone or not watching the group, children waiting unsupervised at the water’s edge, a child being pushed, dunked or held under against their will, no clear answer about who is responsible for safety, or pressure to sign up and pay on the spot. Our policy fine-print checklist lists what to ask about contracts and fees, and switching swim schools helps if you decide to leave a program later.

💬 After Class: Talk, Ask, Compare

Ask your child open questions: “What was the best part?” “Was there anything you did not like?” “Do you want to go back?” Then ask the school the practical questions you could not ask during class: how children are placed in levels, what progress looks like, what it costs in total, how make-up classes work and what happens if you miss a week. Our make-up lesson policies guide and enrollment checklist help with the details. If you tried more than one school, add each total to the comparison box and weigh safety first, then teaching, then your child’s comfort, then price and convenience. For wider advice, see how to choose a swim school.

🧱 A Good Lesson Is One Layer of Protection

A high score tells you a class looks well run. It does not make a child safe around water. Keep a designated water watcher, lock pool gates and doors, and use a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket near open water. Know the warning signs of drowning, which are often quiet, and see the layers of protection checklist. Swim lessons do not make a child drown-proof, at any age.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a free trial swim lesson scorecard?

It is a one-page form you fill in during and after a trial class. You rate the pool deck, the instructor, your child’s comfort and the school’s answers, then tick any red flags, so the decision to enroll rests on what you saw rather than on a first impression.

Should I stay and watch the trial lesson?

If the school allows it, yes. Watching from a spot where you can see the whole group lets you fill in the scorecard and see how your child is treated. Some programs ask parents of older children to wait elsewhere, so ask before the day and follow the school’s rule.

Is one trial class enough to decide?

One class is enough to notice red flags such as poor supervision or a rushed, forceful approach. It cannot show everything, because a child may be tired, shy or excited on the day. If you are unsure, ask whether you can watch a second class or compare a second school with the same scorecard.

What if my child cries or will not get in the water?

That is common and is not a score against your child or the school. What matters is how the instructor responds: a calm pace, small steps, choices for the child and no pressure or forcing. Note the response on the scorecard and talk with your child afterwards.

What if a school does not offer a free trial?

Ask about other ways to see a class before you pay: watching a lesson from the deck, a pool tour, a short assessment or a first-class option. Policies differ by school, so ask what is offered and write the answer on the printable.

Does a high score mean my child will be safe around water?

No. A good lesson is one layer of protection, not a guarantee. Children still need close, undistracted adult supervision, barriers around pools and a properly fitted life jacket near open water, whatever their lesson experience.

How do I compare two schools with this scorecard?

Fill in one scorecard per school and put each total in the comparison box. Look at safety and supervision first, then teaching, then your child’s comfort, then price and convenience. Any ticked red flag deserves a question to the school before you enroll.
