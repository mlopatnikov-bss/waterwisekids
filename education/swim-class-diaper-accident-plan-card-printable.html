Swim Class Diaper & Accident Plan Card

Every baby and toddler swim class has an accident sooner or later, and it is almost never a crisis — if you know what to do. A snug swim diaper, a check about every hour, a quick rinse, and knowing what to do in the first minute keeps your child comfortable, the class running and the water clean for everyone. One page to tape inside the swim bag, plus an eight-class log.

🏠 Before You Leave Home

  • Diaper on before you arrive, or in the changing area — never poolside. Put the swim diaper on at home or in the changing room so the pool deck stays clear of diaper changes.
  • Snug, with a tight leg and waist seal. Gaps at the legs are where leaks start. If you can slide two fingers under the leg band, size up or change style.
  • Layer it: swim diaper first, snug swim pants or a reusable cover over it. Many baby classes require this. Check what yours asks for — and note that neither layer is leak-proof.
  • Bathroom or diaper change right before class. A fresh diaper going in is the single biggest thing you control.
  • Skip the lesson if your child has had diarrhea. See the “Stay Home” box below. This is the rule that protects every other family in the water.

💧 Before Getting In

  • Quick rinse or wipe-down. A short shower, or a wipe of the bottom with a wet cloth, takes off what a diaper cannot hold. Most pools ask for it.
  • Pack the accident kit. Spare swim diapers, a spare cover, wipes, a plastic bag for the used one, a dry towel and a full change of clothes, and hand soap for the walk back.
  • Know where the nearest changing table is. If it is across the building, you want to know before you need it.
  • Tell the instructor if your child has a tummy bug coming. A quiet word beforehand is better than a rushed one from the water.

⏱️ During Class: The 60-Minute Rule

  • Bathroom or diaper check about every 60 minutes. The CDC advises checking diapers often and taking children on bathroom breaks about every hour. Most baby classes are shorter than that, so check just before and just after.
  • Check by touch, not by guess. A swim diaper stays light-looking even when full. Feel the bottom, or peek at the leg band.
  • Change away from the water. Changing in the bathroom or changing area keeps germs off the deck and out of the pool.
  • Toddlers who are potty training: offer, do not force. Ask before every activity, keep the potty trip quick, and stay relaxed. Pressure at the pool makes accidents and lesson dread more likely.
Why the rules exist: the CDC is clear that swim diapers and swim pants are not leak-proof. They may catch some solids, but they do not stop germs from getting into the water. The parasite Cryptosporidium can survive in a properly chlorinated pool for more than a week, and it is a leading cause of swimming-related diarrhea outbreaks.

🚨 If an Accident Happens

  • Stay calm and get out of the water. Your child is not in trouble, and your instructor has handled this before. Wrap your child in a towel first.
  • Tell the instructor or lifeguard straight away. Do not try to scoop or hide it. The pool has a set procedure, and staff need to act while the water is still fresh.
  • Expect the class or pool to pause. If it is a formed stool, staff may remove it and treat the water. If it is diarrhea, the pool may close for a longer clean-up. Both are routine.
  • Clean up in the changing area. Wash your child, bag the diaper, wash your own hands well with soap and water, and dress in dry clothes.
  • Decide whether to come back today. If it was a one-off accident and your child is well, most pools let you return next week. If it was loose or watery, go home.
  • Keep your own reaction small. A child who is scolded at the pool learns to hide accidents. A child who is comforted learns to tell you.
Stay home: diarrhea rule. Do not bring a child to swim with diarrhea — not even a mild case, and not even in a swim diaper. The CDC advises waiting at least 24 hours after diarrhea stops, and if Cryptosporidium is the cause, waiting two weeks after the diarrhea has stopped. If your child has had diarrhea for more than a day, call your pediatrician. Follow your pool’s return-to-swim policy.

🧼 After Class

  • Rinse and dry properly. Rinse off pool water, dry the diaper area and skin folds, and change into a fresh dry diaper. Moisturiser now if your child has dry or eczema-prone skin.
  • Wash hands — yours and your child’s. With soap and water. Sanitiser does not reliably kill Cryptosporidium, so soap and water is the plan after any diaper change.
  • Watch for a tummy bug over the next two weeks. Diarrhea can appear a few days after exposure. If it does, tell your swim school so they can act on it.
  • Rinse the reusable cover; bin the disposable. Do not flush swim diapers. Bag them and put them in the bin.
