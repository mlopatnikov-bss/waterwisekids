Swim Lesson Reluctance Plan

When your child says “I don’t want to go,” check the basics, find the reason, stay calm and kind, and tell the instructor. Keep it in your swim bag.

WaterWiseKids.com — Free water safety education for families

📋 Step 1: Class Basics

Child’s name and age
Current level and instructor
Day, time and pool location
School front desk phone
When the “I don’t want to go” started
What was going on that week

🍎 Step 2: Check the Basics

  • Fed and hydrated? A light snack and a drink, following the school’s rules.
  • Rested? A short rest or quiet time before class if needed.
  • Warm and comfortable? Warm clothes and a dry towel ready for after class.
  • Gear fits? Swimsuit, goggles and hair ties are not rubbing or pinching.
  • Any pain? Ears, skin, eyes or tummy. If something hurts, skip class and call the doctor if needed.

🤔 Step 3: Likely Reason (circle or tick)

  • Tired or hungry. Needs food, rest or a calmer day.
  • Cold or uncomfortable. Needs warmth, a snug suit or a different goggle fit.
  • A skill feels hard. Face in water, floating, going under or a new step.
  • New teacher or group. A change in instructor, class time or friends.
  • Hard goodbye. Worry about being apart from me.
  • Pool is loud or busy. Echoes, crowds or splashing feel like too much.
  • Wants to keep playing. Needs a heads-up and a clear plan for after class.
Not sure? Ask one gentle question: “What part feels hard today?” Then listen without fixing.

💬 Step 4: Scripts to Try

  • Name the feeling. “It sounds like swimming feels hard today.”
  • Offer a small choice. “Do you want the red towel or the blue towel?”
  • Make a tiny plan. “We will go, watch for a minute and see how it feels.”
  • Praise effort. “You tried something new. That is brave.”
  • Plan the after. “After class we will have our snack and read a book.”
Never force, dunk or pull a child into the water. Skip threats, shaming and big bribes. Keep goodbyes short, calm and the same each week.

🧭 Step 5: Notes for the Instructor

QuestionMy notes
What my child says 
What helps (song, game, buddy, a first look from the deck) 
What I notice in class 
Question for the instructor 
Tell the school early if reluctance lasts more than a few classes, or if your child cries, freezes or complains of pain or cold. Ask about pacing, group size and skill steps.

🏠 Step 6: Our Family Plan

Who takes our child to class
Who watches from the deck
Our goodbye words or routine
Our reward for effort (small and predictable)
When we will pause or call the doctor
When we will check in with the instructor
Young children are never alone near water. If a child is in trouble in the water, get help and call 911 or my local emergency number. Lessons do not make a child drown-proof, at any age.

This plan is a family planner, not medical advice. Talk to your child’s doctor about pain or ongoing worry. Follow your school’s policies. Guidance: American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org); American Red Cross.
