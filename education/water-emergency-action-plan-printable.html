Water Emergency Action Plan

Drowning is fast and silent. Decide who does what before anyone gets in the water, and follow the four steps in order: Recognize, Reach, Call, Care.

WaterWiseKids.com — Free water safety education for families

🆘 The Four Steps — In Order

  • 1. RECOGNIZE. Scan constantly. Trouble is quiet: head low with mouth at water level, glassy or closed eyes, no kicking, no sound. Get the person out the instant you suspect it — don't wait to be sure.
  • 2. REACH or THROW. From a stable spot, extend a pole, paddle, branch, or towel — or throw something that floats (ring buoy, cooler, kickboard). Don't go in unless you are trained.
  • 3. CALL. Shout for help and call 911 the moment you sense trouble. Give your exact address and nearest cross street, say it's a drowning, and put the phone on speaker.
  • 4. CARE. Once out of the water: if trained in CPR, begin right away (rescue breaths matter for drowning). If not, the 911 dispatcher will talk you through it until help arrives.

🛟 Rescue Without Becoming a Second Victim

  • Reach or throw, don't go. A panicking person can pull an untrained rescuer under — drowning is a leading cause of death for would-be rescuers.
  • Stay low and braced at the edge so you can't be pulled in.
  • If you must enter, bring a flotation device to keep between you and the person.
  • Keep rescue gear stored at the pool — a reaching pole and a ring buoy — so it's there when seconds count.

👥 Decide These Roles Before You Swim

  • Name a Water Watcher — one adult watching the kids only, no phone or book — and hand the role off in shifts.
  • Agree who calls 911 and where their phone is.
  • Make sure caregivers are CPR-trained — parents, babysitters, and grandparents. Renew about every 2 years.
  • Everyone knows the address and nearest cross street to give a dispatcher.
  • Know where the first-aid kit and rescue gear are kept.
