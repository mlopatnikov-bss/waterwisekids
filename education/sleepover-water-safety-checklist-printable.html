Sleepover Water Safety Checklist

Before your child sleeps over at a friend’s, a relative’s or a rental, confirm who is watching, what water is on the property and what the night-time plan is. Ask early, ask kindly, and keep a plan for your own child either way.

📞 Step 1: Ask the Host Family

  • Is there a pool, hot tub, pond, creek or beach nearby? Include inflatable pools, fountains and large buckets.
  • Is it fenced on all four sides with a self-latching gate? Ask whether doors that open to the pool are alarmed or locked.
  • Will anyone swim, and who will be the designated Water Watcher? One adult, eyes on the water, no phone, no drinks, no chores.
  • Is the pool covered and locked once swimming ends? Ask where the gate key or cover control is kept overnight.
  • Are Coast Guard-approved life jackets available in the right size? If your child is a beginner, pack their own.
  • Who will be awake and who will be in charge at night and early morning? Early risers are a real gap.

🧒 Step 2: Rules Your Child Carries With Them

  • Never go near water without asking an adult first. That includes the pool, hot tub and bathtub, day or night.
  • Wake a grown-up if a friend wants to swim in the dark or early. Say it kindly: “I am not allowed. Let’s go ask.”
  • Never swim alone and never dare a friend. No breath-holding contests or jumping in from the edge.
  • Tell a grown-up your honest swim level. Beginners stay within reach and wear a life jacket.
  • Know the exit word. Pick a family code word your child can text or say to be picked up with no questions asked.

🌙 Step 3: Night-Time and Morning Plan

If a child is missing, check the pool, hot tub and any water first, even if you think they went elsewhere, and call 911 right away. Seconds matter.

🎒 Step 4: Pack and Prepare

  • Pack a snug, properly fitted life jacket if your child is a beginner or non-swimmer.
  • Write the host’s name, address and phone in the plan below, and share your own contact details.
  • Talk through the rules in the car. Two minutes, in your child’s own words.
  • Check the pickup plan. Agree on a time and a code word for an early pickup.
  • If answers are unclear, host at your place instead. A friendly alternative is always an option.
