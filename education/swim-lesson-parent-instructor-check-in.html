Swim Lesson Parent-Instructor Check-In: Questions to Ask and How to Use the Answers (Free Printable)

A two-minute chat with your child’s swim instructor can show you where your child is, what comes next and how to help between classes. Here is when to check in, which questions to ask and a free one-page card to keep the answers.

Quick Answer: Check in briefly at set moments: before class to share what the instructor should know, after class for one specific win and one next step, and mid-session for a skills snapshot. Ask about skills, not just “how did they do,” write the answers down and turn one next step into a short home practice. Our free printable keeps it all on one page.

💬 Why a Quick Check-In Helps

Swim instructors see your child for a short stretch each week, and parents see the rest of the week. Comparing notes helps both sides. The instructor learns what your child is feeling, and you learn which skill is being built, why it matters and how to support it. A good check-in is short, specific and kind. It is not a review of the instructor, and it is not a replacement for the school’s own progress reports. Our guide to evaluating swim instructor feedback shows how to read what you hear, and the after-class debrief checklist helps with the talk you have with your child.

🕒 When to Check In

Pick moments that fit the pool deck. Before class, share one or two quick facts and let the instructor get started. After class, ask for one win and one next step while it is fresh. Every few weeks, or at the school’s progress checkpoint, ask for a skills snapshot by message, report card or scheduled chat. Many schools prefer that longer questions wait until the lesson is over, so ask the front desk how they like to handle it. If a substitute teaches that day, our substitute instructor day checklist covers what to pass along.

🗣️ What to Tell the Instructor

You know your child best. Tell the instructor what helps, such as a favorite song, a fear of water on the face or how your child responds to encouragement. Share changes that matter that day, such as poor sleep, a cold or a rough morning, and mention past water experiences. Put health needs in the school’s official forms too, and use our medical information form to organize them. If your child is nervous, the reluctance plan offers calm scripts to share with the instructor.

❓ Five Questions Worth Asking

Specific questions earn useful answers. Try these: Which skills can my child do on their own now? What is the next skill and why? What is the main thing holding progress back? What can we practice between classes? What would you like to see before the next level? Write the answers on the printable so you can compare them from one check-in to the next. Our swim instructor questions checklist and guide to measuring swimming progress give you more ideas, and realistic progress timelines help you set expectations.

🏠 Turn Answers into Home Practice

Pick one skill from the instructor’s answer and make it a two-minute game. Keep it playful, keep it short, and stay within arm’s reach of your child at all times in or near water, including the bath. Ask the instructor what to practice and what to leave for class, so you do not teach a habit that clashes with the lesson. The goal-setting worksheet and progress audit worksheet help you track what changes. If progress seems stuck, read about a swim lesson plateau before you worry.

🤝 If You Have a Concern

Describe what you saw, ask what the plan is and give the instructor a chance to explain. If you still have questions about level placement, class size or fit, contact the school’s office and ask how requests for a different time or instructor are handled. Our level placement guide, instructor continuity worksheet and re-enrollment decision checklist help you decide what to do next. Any concern about your child’s safety or treatment should go to the school management right away.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to talk to my child’s swim instructor?

Short, planned moments work best. Before class is for quick information, such as a cold, a new fear or a change in routine. After class, ask for one specific win and one next step. Save bigger questions for a mid-session check-in by message, a scheduled chat or a progress report, because the instructor needs to keep watching the pool during class time. Ask your school what it prefers.

What should I ask my child’s swim instructor?

Ask which skills your child can do now, which skill is next, what is holding progress back, what you can practice between classes and when your child may be ready for the next level. Specific questions get specific answers, and the printable card gives you five to start with.

What should I tell the instructor about my child?

Share anything that helps the instructor teach well: past experiences in water, fears, how your child likes to be encouraged, ear or health needs the school should know about, and what happened earlier in the day. Put medical details in the school’s official forms as well, and tell the instructor if something changes.

How do I ask about progress without sounding critical?

Start with what you notice and ask for the instructor’s view, for example “I saw bubbles today. What skill should we practice next?” Thank the instructor for specifics. If you disagree with a level or a plan, ask what skills the school uses to decide and how it measures them, then follow up with the school office if you still have questions.

Can I practice swim skills at home between classes?

Yes, with an adult within arm’s reach of the child at all times in or near water, including the bath. Short, playful practice of the skill the instructor names, such as blowing bubbles or kicking on the steps with support, works better than long drills. Ask the instructor what to practice and what to leave for class.

What if I am not happy with my child’s progress or instructor?

Write down what you have noticed and ask the instructor for the plan first. If concerns remain, contact the school’s office or program manager, ask about class placement, a different instructor or time, and how the school handles these requests. Our guides on evaluating instructor feedback and switching schools can help you decide.
