Swim Lesson Thanksgiving Week Plan: Schedule Changes, Make-Ups and Keeping Skills Fresh (Free Printable)

Holiday weeks can mean closed pools, shortened schedules and travel. Here is how to confirm your class dates, plan make-ups, keep your child’s skills fresh and stay water-safe around gatherings, with a free one-page planner.

Quick Answer: Check your school’s holiday calendar now, confirm which classes are closed or moved, ask how make-ups and credits work, keep skills fresh with short supervised water play and choose a designated water watcher at every gathering. 📅 Why Thanksgiving Week Needs a Plan

The week of Thanksgiving is one of the busiest of the year for families, and it often changes swim lesson schedules. Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26 this year, and some schools close for the day, the days around it or part of the week, while others run normal classes. A few minutes of planning helps you avoid arriving at a closed pool, losing a paid class without knowing your options or letting your child’s routine slip. Use this guide with our make-up tracker, weather cancellation plan and policy fine print checklist.

🗓️ Step 1: Confirm Your Class Dates

Look for your school’s holiday calendar, then confirm the details in writing if you can: which classes are cancelled, which are moved, whether the pool is open for any open swim and how the school will contact you if plans change. Check the dates for the week before and the week after too, because some schools adjust more than one week. Add each class to your calendar with a reminder the evening before, and save the front desk phone number. Our night-before prep checklist and swim lesson day checklist make class days smoother.

🔁 Step 2: Plan Make-Ups and Credits

If a class is closed, ask whether it is made up, credited or simply missed. Find out how long make-up credits last, whether you have to book in advance and whether you can use a different day, time or location. If you will miss a class because of travel, ask before you go rather than after. Write each missed class, the make-up date and any deadline on the planner. Our winter break plan uses the same approach for the longer December break, and the parent-instructor check-in card can help you raise questions with your child’s instructor.

💪 Step 3: Keep Skills Fresh During a Break

A short break is usually fine for a child who is comfortable in the water, but long gaps can make younger children and newer swimmers feel less sure when they return. Keep practice short, playful and supervised. Bath-time bubbles, scooping water without covering the face and practicing a calm breath before water splashes on the face are easy ways to keep water comfort going, and the instructor can suggest skills to practice. A family trip to a pool with lifeguards can help too, as long as an adult stays within arm’s reach of a young child. Home play supports lessons but never replaces them. See our fall swim skill retention checklist and guide to swim skill fade, and review bath time safety for babies and toddlers.

🛡️ Step 4: Water Safety at Gatherings and While Traveling

Holiday gatherings put many adults and children in unfamiliar homes, and busy hosts can lose track of who is watching the kids. Choose a designated water watcher whenever children are near a pool, hot tub, bathtub, pond or open container of water, and swap the role at set times so the person is never distracted by a phone, food or conversation. Keep doors and gates to water closed and latched, empty tubs and buckets after use, and ask hosts and hotels about pool fences and rules. Children are never safe alone near water, and swim lessons do not make a child drown-proof at any age. Our holiday gathering water safety checklist and swim lessons while traveling guide have more steps.

🧣 Step 5: Cold Days, Sick Days and Busy Days

Late November is a time for colds, cold winds and packed schedules. If your child is sick, follow your school’s illness policy, and ask about make-ups before you skip. On class days, bring a warm robe or towel, dress your child in warm layers for the walk to the car and plan a snack. Our sick-day decision guide, cold comfort warm-up checklist, snack and hydration checklist and car seat ride home checklist cover the details. If a child is in trouble in the water, get help and call 911 or your local emergency number.

🏊 Step 6: Easing Back In After the Holiday

Tell the instructor about the break, any travel or illness and how your child seems, and let them warm up at their own pace. Keep the first class back low-pressure, celebrate small wins and avoid comparing your child with others. If skills seem rusty, ask what to practice and how long it usually takes to catch up. Our swim lesson progress plateau guide and frequency guide explain what is normal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do swim lessons run during Thanksgiving week?

It depends on the school. Some schools close for Thanksgiving Day or the days around it, some shorten their schedule and some run as usual. Check your school’s holiday calendar or ask the front desk which dates are affected, how you will be told about changes and whether classes that fall on a closed day are made up.

How do I find out about make-up classes for a holiday closure?

Ask your school before the holiday: whether a closed class is made up or credited, how long make-up credits last, whether you must book a make-up in advance and whether make-ups must be in the same level. Write the answers on the planner and keep an eye on any deadline. Our make-up tracker helps you keep dates and credits in one place.

How long can my child skip swim lessons before losing skills?

There is no single number, because it depends on age, how well a skill is established and how much water time a child gets in between. A short holiday gap is usually not a problem for a child who is comfortable in the water, but younger children and newer swimmers may need a few minutes to warm up when they return. Ask the instructor how to help your child ease back in.

What can we do at home to keep swim skills fresh over the holiday?

Keep it short and playful, and always with a supervising adult within arm’s reach of a young child. Bath-time water play can build comfort with water on the face and blowing bubbles, and a family trip to a pool that has lifeguards and allows children can offer relaxed practice. Home practice supports lessons, but it does not replace them or the instructor’s guidance.

How do I keep kids water-safe at holiday gatherings and while traveling?

Choose a designated adult to watch children near any water, such as a pool, hot tub, bathtub, pond or even a bucket, and swap that role at set times so no one is distracted. Keep doors and gates to water closed and latched, empty tubs and buckets after use, and ask hosts and hotels about pool rules and fencing. Our holiday gathering water safety checklist has more steps.

What if my child is sick or the weather is bad on a lesson day this week?

Follow your school’s sick-day and weather policies, and ask about make-ups before you skip. If your child has a fever or is vomiting or has diarrhea, most schools ask families to stay home. Our sick-day decision guide and weather cancellation plan walk through the questions to ask.
