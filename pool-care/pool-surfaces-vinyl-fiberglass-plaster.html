Vinyl, Fiberglass or Plaster: Caring for Your Pool's Surface

How care differs for vinyl liners, fiberglass and plaster or aggregate pools: brushing, chemistry, staining, draining risks and when to call a pro.

  • Guides
  • Pool stores
  • Service companies

Applies to: In-ground and above-ground pools with vinyl liners, in-ground fiberglass pools, and plaster, concrete or aggregate finishes. Surface makers' instructions differ; your own manual and warranty come first.

Quick answer

Every surface needs balanced water, but makers give different targets and cautions. Vinyl liners can bleach where undissolved chlorine sits and can be damaged by draining. On River Pools fiberglass, the concern is calcium combined with high pH, and on Pebble Tec finishes a nylon brush is very important. Follow your own surface maker's guide.[1][2][3][4]

Pool upkeep is not drowning prevention.Keep the fence, self-latching gate, alarms and close adult supervision in place, and keep children away from chemicals and equipment while you work. See backyard pool safety layers.

Why surface type changes the routine

Owning a pool finish comes with the responsibility of keeping water chemistry right. Improper or corrosive water can damage surfaces, equipment and decks.[1][5]

Water chemistry can go wrong in both directions: corrosive water dissolves plaster walls, and over-saturated water causes scaling and staining.[6]

Makers do not agree on every number. For calcium hardness, River Pools' fiberglass guide gives less than 120 ppm, while Latham's fiberglass warranty lists 250–350 ppm. Use the figures from your own pool's maker.[4][5]

Vinyl liners

  • Brush gently. For Latham liners, a soft-bristle brush or sponge is suggested to keep dirt and undissolved chemicals from building up on the liner.[3]
  • Watch the brush ends. On Continental liners, sharp brush ends can tear the liner, especially in corners and on steps; use a vacuum head designed for vinyl pools.[7]
  • Keep chlorine off the liner. For Continental liners, do not put tablets or sticks in the skimmer, and never broadcast dry chlorine directly into the pool. Both can bleach the vinyl.[7]
  • Watch chlorine levels. Excess chlorine is the most common cause of fading. The vinyl liner makers' association (CFFA) gives a recommended 1.0–3.0 ppm. CDC's minimums still apply: at least 1 ppm in pools, or at least 2 ppm if you use cyanuric acid (stabilizer).[2][8]
  • Avoid spot bleaching. It is linked to undissolved product settling, floating dispensers resting in one spot, and too little brushing after shocking.[2]
  • Clean the waterline gently. Dirt and oils there can stain, so don't use abrasive cleaners, and check with your pool maker before using any chemical on the liner.[3][7]
  • No sharp objects or toys with sharp edges, which can puncture or tear the liner.[3][7]

Some liner makers tell owners to dissolve chlorine or shock in a bucket first. Pre-dissolve only when the product label directs it, and then add the chemical to water. Follow your product label.[3][9]

SafetyNever empty a vinyl liner pool on your own. A liner can shrink and crack if the pool is emptied. Continental liners must not be fully drained without professional advice.[3][7]

Fiberglass pools

River Pools' fiberglass guide: calcium is the root cause of most discoloration on fiberglass, and excess calcium combined with high pH and chlorine can discolor the gel coat.[4]

  • In River Pools' guidance, avoid calcium hypochlorite in fiberglass pools and do not add calcium.[4]
  • In River Pools' guidance, also use a chelating stain, metal and scale control product in fiberglass pools. River Pools' fiberglass ranges differ from industry standards built around concrete pools.[4]
  • Latham fiberglass pools are designed to stay full of water at all times. The Latham warranty excludes damage from improper draining or rising groundwater.[5]
  • For Latham fiberglass pools, keep runoff water away from the pool. The warranty requires weekly water testing by a pool professional.[5]

Plaster and aggregate finishes: one finish maker's guidance

Pebble Tec aggregate finishes are made of pebbles and cement. For them, test the water chemistry at least once a week and keep printed test reports in case of a warranty request. That is separate from daily checks: check chlorine and pH at least twice per day.[1][10]

  • On Pebble Tec finishes, brush with a nylon brush (very important), working the walls and floor toward the main drain.[1]
  • On Pebble Tec finishes, losing a few pebbles during start-up is normal.[1]
  • High chlorine can make metals or minerals drop out of the water and cause cloudiness, scale or stains.[1]
  • For a new Pebble Tec finish, wait at least 14 days before heating the water, and monitor chemical balance more closely after you turn on the heater.[1]
  • Salt can harm new or unprotected finishes. On Pebble Tec finishes, salt left on the surface may scale, stain or discolor it. With Pentair IntelliChlor salt systems, some salt pellets could damage plaster. Waiting periods before adding salt differ by maker.[1][6][11]

With a Pebble Tec finish, follow its instructions where they differ from general advice, since they are specialized to its products. Check what your own finish maker says.[1]

Stains: identify before you treat

Most vinyl stains are organic or metal. Metal stains often come from ladders or heaters, and waterline rings from undissolved chemicals or calcium. Contact the pool maker before using chemicals on a liner.[3]

SafetyStain products are still pool chemicals. Read the label, wear goggles and gloves, keep children away, and never mix products.[7][9][10]

When to call a professional

When to call a professionalCall a pool professional before draining a vinyl or fiberglass pool. Latham's vinyl guidance also points to a professional before draining, Continental liners must not be fully drained without professional advice, and Latham's fiberglass warranty excludes damage from improper draining.[3][5][7]

When to call a professionalGet help with liner leaks and persistent wrinkles. Small leaks can cause wrinkles, and wrinkles can also come from installation or chemistry problems. Persistent wrinkles may need a professional.[3]

When to call a professionalIf a fiberglass pool is losing water through a crack or fracture, contact the manufacturer. Latham's warranty defines structural failure as water loss due to fracture or crack.[5]

Common questions

Can I use the same brush on every pool?
Check your maker's guidance; it differs. For vinyl, Latham suggests a soft-bristle brush, and on Continental liners sharp brush ends can tear the liner. Pebble Tec specifies nylon for its finishes.[1][3][7]
Why does my vinyl liner have pale spots?
Localized fading is linked to improper shocking and to undissolved product or floating dispensers resting in one area.[2]
Can I drain my pool to clean it?
Not without advice. Vinyl liners can shrink and crack when emptied, and Latham fiberglass pools are designed to stay full.[3][5]
