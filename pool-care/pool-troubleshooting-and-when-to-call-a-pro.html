Pool Troubleshooting, and When to Call a Pro

A calm symptom-to-cause guide for common home-pool problems: what owners can safely check, and the clear signs it is time to call a qualified pro.

  • Guides
  • Pool stores
  • Service companies

Applies to: In-ground and above-ground home pools with a pump, filter, heater or salt system. Checks differ by model, so your own equipment manual comes first.

Quick answer

Many pool problems trace back to a few causes: a suction-side air leak, a dirty filter, low flow or water chemistry. Stick to simple owner checks; equipment manuals leave panels, wiring and heater repairs to qualified technicians. A GFCI that trips by itself, a gas smell or a broken drain cover means stop and get help.[1][2][3][4][5][6][7]

Pool upkeep is not drowning prevention.Keep the fence, self-latching gate, alarms and close adult supervision in place, and keep children away from chemicals and equipment while you work. See backyard pool safety layers.

Safe checks only

Owner checks in equipment manuals are mostly visual: baskets, valve settings, the filter gauge. Your own manual is the authority for your model.[2][6][8]

SafetyNever work on live electrical equipment. On a Hayward Super Pump, turn off power before working on the pump or motor; damaged wiring must be replaced immediately; and the pump should be installed and serviced only by a qualified professional. On Hayward Universal H Series heaters and AquaRite salt systems, only qualified technicians should remove the panel.[5][6][9]

Pump won’t prime or loses prime

Possible causes on a Hayward pump: an air leak on the suction side, a blocked suction line, or an impeller broken off the motor shaft.[1]

  1. With the power off (Hayward Super Pump models require this before any work on the pump), make sure the strainer housing is full of water and the lid O-ring is clean and seated.[6]
  2. Clean the strainer and skimmer baskets, then refill the strainer housing.[6]
  3. Watch the return fittings. On any self-priming pump, suction air leaks show up as bubbles there.[6]

SafetyDon’t let the pump run dry. Hayward pumps should never operate without water; the seal can overheat and melt.[10]

When to call a professionalOn a Hayward Super Pump, a clogged suction side or a jammed impeller needs a qualified repair professional.[6]

Low flow or high filter pressure

For low flow on a Hayward pump, check the filter first: when filter pressure rises over the clean starting pressure, it is time to backwash or clean it. Algae can also clog a filter and keep pressure high, and the filter cannot kill algae. Sand and D.E. filters are backwashed per the maker’s instructions and cartridges are cleaned or replaced, so check your own filter’s manual for when and how.[2][6]

Above-ground systems differ. On a Bestway sand filter pump, no flow can come from a blocked strainer, a valve left on Closed, or trapped air.[8]

When to call a professionalOn Hayward equipment, a blocked or plugged return line is a dealer job.[2]

Heater won’t fire

Start with flow. If a Jandy JXi heater does not start, check that the pump is on, the filter is clean and water is flowing. Never run a Hayward Universal H Series heater below minimum flow.[3][5]

SafetySmell gas? These steps come from Jandy’s heater manual: don’t try to light any appliance, don’t touch electric switches or use a phone in the building, and call your gas supplier from a neighbor’s phone, or the fire department if you can’t reach them.[3]

When to call a professionalJandy JXi heaters: have a qualified service technician do all service, and do not use one that has been under water. For a Hayward Universal H Series heater showing an error, contact a local authorized service center; a failed Hayward heat exchanger must be inspected by a Hayward service company.[3][5][11]

Salt cell warning lights

On a Hayward AquaRite, flashing Check Salt and Inspect Cell lights mean low salt; solid lights mean chlorine production has stopped. A dirty AquaRite cell can misread salt, so confirm with an independent test. Its No Flow light means the flow switch isn’t seeing steady flow. A Pentair IntelliChlor stops producing chlorine in cold water.[9][12]

SafetyNever run a salt cell without flow; on a Pentair IntelliChlor, flammable gases can build up. If your manual calls for cleaning the cell with acid, follow it exactly. On Hayward’s AquaRite, for example, wear eye protection and gloves, and always add acid to water, never water to acid.[9][12]

When to call a professionalA Pentair IntelliChlor salt system should be serviced only by a qualified pool service professional.[12]

Losing water

Leaks can be in the liner, pump seal, piping or pool-to-pipe connection. Losing more than 2 inches a week (3 in hot, dry areas) likely means a leak. Wet spots and bubbles in the return line are warning signs too. Try a bucket test, explained in our water conservation guide.[13]

When to call a professionalCracks in the pool shell, plumbing or fittings need repair. Be careful about draining to look for a leak: an empty pool can pop out of the ground where the water table is high, and fiberglass or in-ground vinyl liner pools should never be empty.[14][15]

When a GFCI trips

A GFCI is the best safety device to prevent electrocution. If the GFCI protecting a Hayward Super Pump's supply circuit, or the GFCI provided with a Bestway 58692E sand filter pump, cuts power to the pump without the test button being pushed, a ground current is flowing and a shock is possible: stop using the pump and have a qualified service representative fix it.[6][8][16]

  • Test GFCIs as your manual says. The general advice is monthly; on the Bestway 58692E above-ground sand filter pump, the GFCI must be tested before each use.[8][16]
  • Know where the breakers for pool equipment are and how to shut them off in an emergency.[16]
  • Do not use an extension cord with a Hayward Super Pump.[6]

SafetyNever bypass a GFCI to keep equipment running. It is designed to protect people from severe or fatal shocks.[17]

When to call a professionalHave an electrician qualified in pool and spa repairs inspect and upgrade your pool wiring.[16]

Strong “chlorine” smell or stinging eyes

A “chlorine” smell is usually chloramines, formed when chlorine combines with sweat, dirt and pee. They can irritate eyes and lungs. Test the water: keep pH 7.0–7.8 and chlorine at least 1 ppm in pools, or at least 2 ppm if you use cyanuric acid (stabilizer) or chlorine products that contain it.[4][18]

SafetyIf someone breathes in chemical fumes, get them to fresh air and call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222. A gas smell near the heater calls for the gas steps above.[3][19]

Stains

With a Jandy JXi heater, acidic water can carry metal that may show up as brown stains on the finish of the pool bottom. If you use a copper-based algicide, you must keep proper water balance and the right algicide concentration to prevent staining pool surfaces.[3][20]

When to call a professionalOn a Pentair IntelliChlor system, have a local pool professional check for metals and recommend removal.[12]

Stop and call a pro

  • Electrical: a GFCI that trips by itself, damaged wiring, anything behind a panel.[5][6]
  • Gas: any gas smell, a heater that still won’t fire after flow checks, or one that has been under water.[3]
  • Structure: cracks, or water loss you can’t trace. Don’t empty the pool on your own; an empty pool can pop out of the ground where the water table is high.[14][15]
  • Chemical exposure: Poison Help, 1-800-222-1222, free and confidential, any time. Not breathing: call 911.[21][22]
  • A loose, broken or missing drain cover: nobody swims until it is fixed.[7]
  • Anything your manual reserves for a professional, such as Hayward’s pump service or a malfunctioning Zodiac robotic cleaner.[6][23]

Common questions

Why does my pump get hot and shut off?
For a Hayward pump, check that the motor’s air vents are clear of debris and that the pump sits where water drains away. Undersized wiring or a poor connection can also cause it; on a Hayward Super Pump, have a qualified professional check the wiring.[6][24]
