How to Close Your Pool for Winter

Close a home pool for winter or keep it running in a mild climate: manufacturer steps, safety covers vs debris covers, and when to call a pro.

  • Guides
  • Pool stores
  • Service companies

Applies to: In-ground and above-ground home pools in freezing and mild-winter climates; pumps, filters, heaters, heat pumps and covers

Quick answer

Whether you close depends on your climate. If you get steady snow, sleet or temperatures around or below 32°F, winterize. Sun Belt pools usually stay open unless a cold front is coming. Follow your own equipment manuals to drain and protect gear. Not every cover is a safety cover, so keep fences, gates and alarms working all winter.[1][2][3][4][5]

Pool upkeep is not drowning prevention.Keep the fence, self-latching gate, alarms and close adult supervision in place, and keep children away from chemicals and equipment while you work. See backyard pool safety layers.

Do you need to close your pool?

There is no single closing date that fits every home. If you get a consistent amount of snow, sleet, or temperatures around or below 32°F (0°C), you need to winterize. In the Sun Belt, pools usually stay open all year unless there's a chance of a cold front.[1][2]

Your local forecast helps with timing. A National Weather Service Freeze Watch means significant, widespread freezing temperatures are possible within the next 24 to 36 hours.[6]

  • Where pools stay open, keep the water level the same so water can keep circulating through the skimmers.[2]
  • Consider removing robotic cleaners and booster pumps in case of nightly frost.[2]
  • Have a freeze protection plan for a winter storm or power outage. Our freeze-protection guide covers this.[2]

Before you start: chemicals and children

Balance your water before winterizing. Use only the products and amounts on each label for your pool's volume.[1][7]

SafetyRead the label before each use, and wear safety goggles and gloves. Never mix chlorine products with acid, and do not mix different chemicals, such as different types of chlorine products. Keep incompatible chemicals, such as acid and chlorine, separate, and lock chemicals up to protect people and animals. Guidance for pool operators also calls for securing chemical storage to limit access, especially for children and animals. Need help now? Call the Poison Help Line at 1-800-222-1222.[7][8][9][10]

In-ground pools: protecting the equipment

Work from your own manual for each piece of equipment. Steps naming a maker or model apply to that product.

  1. Remove cleaners, booster pumps and accessories from the pool.[1]
  2. Turn power off at the circuit breaker before draining anything. The Pentair IntelliFlo3 winterizing steps start with disconnecting all power at the circuit breaker; on a Hayward Max-Flo pump, turn off power to the motor first to avoid a dangerous or fatal shock.[11][12]
  3. Lower the water as your pool professional advises. A lower level helps with freeze protection and overflow. Fiberglass and in-ground vinyl liner pools should never be empty.[1][13]
  4. Drain the pump by following every step of your own pump manual's winterizing procedure, in order. On a Pentair IntelliFlo3, the steps include removing both drain plugs from the bottom of the strainer pot and draining the pump. On a Hayward Max-Flo, drain all water from the pump and piping when freezing temperatures are expected.[11][12]
  5. Drain the filter. For a Hayward S200 sand filter, drain the tank through the drain cap and leave the cap off during winter, then winterize the pump per its own instructions. If you have a cartridge or DE filter, use that filter's manual.[3]
  6. Have a pool professional clear the lines. A pool expert will blow out the lines and plug the skimmer. In a system with a Hayward Max-Flo pump, purging with compressed air can make components explode.[1][12]
  7. Protect the motor without sealing it in. Do not wrap a Pentair IntelliFlo3 motor or a Hayward Max-Flo pump in plastic for winter storage.[11][12]
  8. Store removable equipment somewhere dry.[1]

When to call a professionalJandy JXi gas heaters: where freezing occurs and the pool won't be used, the pool should be winterized by a qualified pool service technician. A Pentair IntelliFlo3 pump must be installed by a licensed or certified electrician or a qualified professional. Never open its drive motor enclosure.[11][14]

Above-ground pools: check your model

Bestway's owner's manual for APX 365, Steel Pro MAX and Power Steel pools gives winter steps only for APX 365 pools, which can stay up in winter without emptying all the water. If you own another brand, such as Intex, follow that pool's own manual.[4]

  • On a Bestway APX 365 pool, disconnect the filtration system and other accessories from the pool.[4]
  • At around 32°F (0°C) or below, the water can freeze. On Bestway APX 365 pools, water left above the inlet and outlet may freeze inside and damage the liner, so follow the manual's water-level step.[4]
  • If ice forms in a Bestway APX 365 pool, remove it very carefully. Sharp edges of the ice can damage the liner.[4]
  • Consider an air pillow for an above-ground pool to relieve pressure from rain, snow and ice.[1]

Taking down a Bestway APX 365, Steel Pro MAX or Power Steel pool? Drain it away from the house and anything that could be damaged by water, check local regulations for drainage, and make sure the liner and accessories are completely clean and dry before storage. If it stays up, remove or secure the ladder to prevent entry when the pool is not in use.[4]

Covers: safety cover or debris cover?

Power safety covers should conform to the ASTM F1346 standard, which sets safety performance requirements for pool covers to protect young children from drowning. As an older CPSC publication describes it, ASTM requires a safety cover to withstand the weight of two adults and a child, to allow a rescue.[15][16]

Not every cover is a safety cover, so check what yours is made for. For example, the cover in Bestway's APX 365, Steel Pro MAX and Power Steel manual is not designated as a safety barrier.[4]

SafetyWater on a cover is a drowning hazard. The safety-cover standard requires quick removal of water from the cover, and a young child can drown in just inches of water. Check the cover for holes or abrasions. Keep the control for a powered cover out of children's reach.[1][15][16]

Keep every barrier working all winter

  • The water should only be reachable through a self-closing, self-latching gate.[5]
  • A gate that fails to close and latch completely is the weak link in any fence. Never prop a pool gate open.[15][16]
  • Keep door alarms and pool covers in working order.[5]
  • Barriers are not childproof. Never leave a child unsupervised near a pool.[15][16]

When to call a professional

When to call a professionalCall a qualified technician for gas heaters, for blowing out lines, and whenever a manual says so. On a Hayward HP50CLEE, HP65CLEE or HP75CLEE heat pump, only a certified technician should service the refrigerant system. Under the GFCI procedure in the Hayward S200 sand filter manual, a GFCI that fails its test is defective: disconnect the electrical equipment and have a qualified service representative correct the problem before using it.[1][3][14][17]

Common questions

Can I just drain my in-ground pool for the winter?
Fiberglass and in-ground vinyl liner pools should never be empty. Where the water table is high, an empty pool can pop out of the ground. Lower the water only as your manual or pool professional advises.[2][13]
Is a winter cover enough to keep my kids safe?
Not by itself. Some covers, like the one in Bestway's APX 365, Steel Pro MAX and Power Steel manual, are not designated as safety barriers, and a young child can drown in just inches of water. Barriers are not childproof. Never leave a child unsupervised near a pool, and keep the fence, self-closing, self-latching gate and alarms working too.[4][5][15][16]
