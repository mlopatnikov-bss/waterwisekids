Your Home Pool Care Routine, Week by Week

A calm weekly and monthly routine for family pools: what to test, clean and check, why it matters, and how it changes with your pool and equipment.

  • Guides
  • Pool stores
  • Service companies

Applies to: In-ground and above-ground home pools with vinyl, fiberglass or plaster surfaces; chlorine and salt-chlorine systems; sand, cartridge and DE filters.

Quick answer

Test chlorine and pH at least twice a day, and more often when lots of people are swimming. Each week, test, skim, brush, vacuum, empty the skimmer and pump baskets and check the water level. Monthly, clean the filter as needed and inspect your equipment, always following your own manuals.[1][2]

Pool upkeep is not drowning prevention.Keep the fence, self-latching gate, alarms and close adult supervision in place, and keep children away from chemicals and equipment while you work. See backyard pool safety layers.

Why a routine matters

Chlorine and pH are the first defense against germs that can make swimmers sick. As a home pool owner, it is your job to check them regularly.[1]

Keeping a pool clean, safe and swim-ready takes regular upkeep. The checklists here are general guidance, so always follow your manufacturers' recommendations and local rules.[2]

SafetyPool care keeps water healthy, but it is not a drowning-prevention layer. Provide continuous, close supervision and four-sided fencing that separates the pool from the house, with self-closing and self-latching gates.[3]

Every day: chlorine and pH

Test chlorine and pH at least twice a day, and more often when the pool is busy.[1][3]

Keep pool pH at 7.0–7.8 and chlorine at least 1 ppm. If you use cyanuric acid (stabilizer) or stabilized chlorine such as dichlor or trichlor, keep chlorine at least 2 ppm.[1]

Test chlorine routinely, because many things use it up: pee, poop, sweat and dirt from swimmers, plus sunlight.[1]

Also test after a party or heavy use, after animals have been in the pool, after heavy rain and after fertilizing.[4]

For how to test and read results, see our guide to testing pool water.

Your weekly checklist

Do these tasks each week:[2]

  • Test the water: check chlorine, pH, alkalinity and other levels, and adjust as needed. Read and follow all directions on product labels.[2][3]
  • Skim: clear leaves, bugs and floating debris with a skimmer net.[2]
  • Brush walls and floor to help prevent algae and stains. Algae can follow poor circulation, which leaves dead zones of still water.[2][4]
  • Vacuum dirt and debris from the floor.[2]
  • Empty the skimmer and pump baskets so water keeps flowing. These baskets are your first line of defense for a clean pool.[2][5]
  • Check the water level. It matters for efficient circulation.[2]

Add a quick safety walk-around to the same day:

  • Look at drain covers. Never enter a pool or spa with a loose, broken or missing drain cover.[6]
  • Test the gate. The water should only be reachable through a self-closing, self-latching gate.[6]
  • Check covers and alarms. Have a door alarm from the house to the pool area, and keep pool covers in working order.[6]

Your monthly checklist

Once a month, take care of the filter, shock and equipment:[2]

  • Clean the filter as needed: backwash sand filters or clean cartridges. Backwashing is used for sand and DE filters, but a cartridge filter cannot be backwashed.[2][5]
  • Use the pressure gauge. It is a benchmark for when to clean any filter type; check your filter's manual for the reading that means it's time.[2][5]
  • Shock the pool to oxidize contaminants and keep water clear. Use the product label for your pool's volume.[2][3]
  • Inspect pumps, filters and heaters for wear or damage.[2]

It is also a good idea to have a professional pool expert check your water once a month.[7]

Every quarter, test total dissolved solids (TDS) and clean tile and grout.[2]

Keep a pool log of test results and maintenance. It helps you see how your water changes and spot recurring problems.[2][8]

How your routine changes with your pool

  • Filters: identify which filter type you have before cleaning it, because the process differs. Your filter's manual is the final word.[2][5]
  • Vinyl liners: both low and high pH can damage a liner. Below pH 7.0 a liner can develop wrinkles, and high pH speeds up its aging.[7]
  • Salt systems: salt chlorine generators use electricity and dissolved salt to make chlorine. Different generators need different salt levels, and scale forms inside the cell even in balanced water. Follow your maker's instructions for salt and cell cleaning.[9]
  • Heaters: for Hayward Universal H-Series heaters, only qualified technicians should remove the panel, and you should contact an authorized service center for service.[10]
  • Above-ground pools: follow the manufacturer's instructions for your pool and its equipment, since the checklists here are general guidance.[2]

Chemical and equipment safety

SafetyMishandling pool chemicals can cause serious injuries. Read and follow every label, wear gloves and goggles, and keep chemicals secure and away from children and pets.[3]

SafetyNever mix chlorine products with acid; this could create toxic gases. Never mix different pool chemicals, such as different types of chlorine products, either. Store acid and chlorine apart, keep chemicals dry, cool and out of direct sunlight, and keep them closed in their original labeled containers.[11][12]

SafetyPool covers: drain standing water from the pool cover. Even a few inches of water can be hazardous, especially to young children.[13]

SafetyElectrical equipment: hazardous voltage can shock, burn or cause serious injury. On Hayward AquaRite salt systems, only qualified technicians should remove the panel.[14]

When to call a professionalCall a qualified professional for electrical or control-panel problems, gas heater faults, and leaks. On Hayward AquaRite salt systems and Hayward Universal H-Series gas heaters, only qualified technicians should remove the panel. Cracks in the shell, plumbing or fittings can cause water loss that needs repair. If someone is exposed to a pool chemical, call the Poison Help Line at 1-800-222-1222.[10][14][15][16]

As the season winds down

When you open and close the pool, balance the water, inspect equipment and winterize when needed.[2]

When and how you close depends on your climate and equipment, so follow your manufacturers' instructions. Our guides to closing your pool and freeze protection walk through it step by step, and the pool closing safety checklist covers family safety.

Common questions

How often should I test my pool?
Test chlorine and pH at least twice a day, and more often when many people are swimming. Also test after heavy use, animals in the pool, heavy rain or fertilizing.[1][4]
If the water looks clear, can I skip a test?
No. Clear water doesn't guarantee clean water, and chlorine must be tested routinely because many things lower it.[1][17]
Does a well-kept pool mean kids are safe?
Water care and drowning prevention are separate jobs. Provide continuous, close supervision and a four-sided fence with self-closing, self-latching gates.[3]
