Home Pool Equipment Explained

What home pool equipment does: pumps, filters, heaters, salt systems, cleaners, covers and drain covers, plus the jobs that need a licensed pro.

  • Guides
  • Pool stores
  • Service companies

Applies to: In-ground and above-ground home pools; vinyl, fiberglass and plaster finishes; chlorine and salt systems; gas, heat pump and solar heating

Quick answer

Home pools rely on a pump and filter to move and clean the water. Some also have a heater, a salt chlorine generator, an automatic cleaner or a cover. Power safety covers and anti-entrapment drain covers are safety equipment, but swimmers under part of a solar cover can be unable to breathe. Gas and electrical work belongs to trained, qualified professionals.[1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8]

Pool upkeep is not drowning prevention.Keep the fence, self-latching gate, alarms and close adult supervision in place, and keep children away from chemicals and equipment while you work. See backyard pool safety layers.

The equipment at a glance

  • Pump: re-circulates water through the filter to keep it clear and hygienic.[1]
  • Filter: sand, cartridge or DE, each cleaned differently.[9][10][11]
  • Heater: gas, electric heat pump or solar thermal.[1]
  • Chlorinator: Hayward's electronic chlorinator is a salt chlorine generator that creates free chlorine.[2][12]
  • Automatic cleaner: robotic, or powered by the pool pump or a booster pump.[1]
  • Covers and drain covers: pool covers and anti-entrapment drain covers are among the safety devices you can install. Power safety covers should meet ASTM F1346; a solar cover is a different product (see the covers section below).[3][4][5]

Check that equipment is meant for your pool type. Hayward pumps are intended for permanently installed pools, not storable pools, and one Bestway above-ground sand filter pump is for storable pools only.[9][13]

SafetyEquipment should never become a ladder. From the Hayward ProSeries sand filter manual: filters, pumps and heaters must be placed so young children cannot use them to reach the pool. Place barriers so equipment cannot be used to climb them.[3][9]

Pumps and filters

Variable-speed and multi-speed pumps can help cut energy costs. An oversized pump makes the filter less effective. Have a professional contractor help select and install your pump.[1][14]

Our pump and filter guides cover the details, including strainer baskets, pressure gauges and cleaning steps.

Heaters: gas, heat pump and solar

  • Gas: gas heaters burn gas and can run in almost any climate, at almost any time of year. They suit pools that need occasional rapid heating.[1][15]
  • Heat pump: a heat pump moves heat from the surrounding air into the water, so it needs warmth in the air to work. Hayward's own heat pumps cut off in roughly 45 to 50 degree F air.[15][16]
  • Heat pump and solar: both are good at holding a pool in a set temperature range over long periods but are very sensitive to climate.[1]

Covering the pool when it is not in use can cut heating costs; most heat is lost in the evening through evaporation. If you use a solar cover, never leave it partly in place (see the covers section below). Pool heater makers have had to meet federal U.S. Department of Energy conservation standards since 1990.[1][5][17][18]

SafetyHayward gas heaters should be diagnosed and repaired by trained personnel, and a fuel conversion needs a licensed or certified gas professional. The venting and clearance rules in your heater's manual must be met. On Hayward Universal H-Series heaters, only qualified technicians should remove the panel, because of hazardous voltage.[6][19][20]

Chlorinators and salt systems

With a Hayward Aqua Rite salt system, consult a pool professional about your pool's specific needs. Its cell should be inspected, and cleaned if needed, every three months or at start-up. Check your own system's manual.[21][22]

A salt system does not replace water testing. Check the chlorine level and pH at least twice a day, and more often when the pool is busy.[23]

SafetyOn Bestway's above-ground sand filter pump, do not put chlorine in the strainer. Mishandling pool chemicals can cause serious injuries. Follow label directions, wear protective gear, and keep chemicals away from children and pets.[13][23]

Automatic cleaners

Robotic cleaners run on low-voltage power and collect debris in built-in filters, instead of relying on the pool pump or a booster pump. A pump-powered cleaner takes about twice the flow that filtering does.[1]

Covers: safety covers vs solar covers

Power safety covers can serve as a barrier. They should conform to ASTM F1346, a standard for pool covers that protect young children from drowning.[3]

A solar cover is different. It is a buoyant plastic cover that floats on the water to heat it, and swimmers under part of one risk surfacing into it and being unable to breathe. Never leave one partly in place, and never swim in a partly covered pool.[5]

SafetyBarriers are not child proof, but they provide layers of protection. Keep pool covers well maintained and their control devices out of children's reach. Fences, barriers, alarms and covers can be lifesaving devices. A cover is only one layer: for supervision, fences and alarms, see our backyard pool safety pages.[3][24]

Drain covers and the Virginia Graeme Baker Act

The Virginia Graeme Baker Pool and Spa Safety Act is a federal law for public pools and spas. Home pools are not required to add anti-entrapment drain covers, but it is recommended. Hair, limbs, jewelry or swimsuits can get stuck in a drain, and suction can even trap an adult.[3][4][24]

  • Never enter a pool or spa with a loose, broken or missing drain cover.[24]
  • From the Hayward ProSeries sand filter manual: keep pool and spa suction outlets clear of debris. Suction outlet parts have a finite life; inspect covers often and replace them at least every ten years or if found damaged. Check your own cover's label too.[9]
  • Not sure your drain covers are anti-entrapment? Ask your pool service representative.[3]

When to call a professionalA pool with a broken, loose or missing drain cover should be closed immediately until a licensed professional makes repairs.[25]

Jobs for licensed or qualified professionals

When to call a professionalCall a pro for gas heater diagnosis, repair, venting or fuel conversion; electrical wiring, bonding and GFCI problems; heater panel or heat exchanger work; installing or servicing equipment whose manual requires a qualified professional; and drain cover repairs.[6][7][8][11][19][20][25][26]

Common questions

Is a solar cover a safety cover?
Do not assume so. A solar cover is a buoyant plastic cover that floats on the water, and swimmers can surface under one and be unable to breathe. For child-drowning protection, look for covers that conform to ASTM F1346, which also sets labeling requirements for all pool covers.[3][5]
Does the Virginia Graeme Baker Act cover my backyard pool?
The federal law requires anti-entrapment drain covers in public pools and spas. Residential pools are not required to install them, but it is recommended. Check your state and local rules too.[3]
Can I repair a gas pool heater myself?
Hayward gas heaters should be diagnosed and repaired by trained personnel, and a fuel conversion needs a licensed or certified gas professional.[6]
Do I still need to test the water with a salt system?
Yes. Check chlorine and pH at least twice a day, and more often when the pool is busy.[23]
