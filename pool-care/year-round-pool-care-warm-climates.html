Keeping Your Pool Running Year-Round in a Warm Climate

Keep a pool running all year in a hot or mild climate: sun and chlorine, algae, chemical storage in heat, hurricane prep and lightning.

  • Guides
  • Pool stores
  • Service companies

Applies to: In-ground and above-ground pools in hot or mild climates that stay open all year; chlorine and salt-chlorine systems; pools with or without a heater. Check your own equipment manual for anything specific to your filter, pump, heater or chlorinator.

Quick answer

Sunlight is one of the things that reduce chlorine. Test chlorine and pH at least twice a day; in a pool that never closes, that routine runs all year. Florida extension advice for hurricanes: do not drain the pool before the storm. When you hear thunder, go indoors.[1][2][3]

Pool upkeep is not drowning prevention.Keep the fence, self-latching gate, alarms and close adult supervision in place, and keep children away from chemicals and equipment while you work. See backyard pool safety layers.

How sun and heat change your water

Sunlight reduces chlorine, and so do sweat and dirt from swimmers. Test chlorine and pH at least twice a day, and more often when many people swim.[1]

Keep pH between 7.0 and 7.8 and chlorine at least 1 ppm in pools. If you use cyanuric acid, a chlorine stabilizer, or chlorine products that contain it (often sold as dichlor or trichlor), keep chlorine at least 2 ppm. Disinfection takes longer when cyanuric acid is present.[1]

Chlorine works less well as pH rises, especially above 8.0. Follow your test kit maker's directions.[1]

Evaporation is a leading cause of pool water loss, especially in hot, drier climates. Warmer water evaporates faster.[4]

Algae and debris pressure

In a pool that never closes, upkeep cannot take a season off. Untreated or undertreated water will result in algae growth.[5]

Bestway above-ground pools: if the water turns green, restore the chemical balance with help from a local pool chemical dealer.[6]

  • Choose landscape plants that won't add more debris to the pool. Shade from landscaping can also keep the water cooler and reduce evaporation.[4]
  • Do not pressure-wash into the pool. There are no chemical treatments to remove fertilizers from pool water.[2]

Store chemicals for the heat

SafetyGuidance written for pool operators: store pool chemicals below 95°F (35°C), out of direct sunlight, protected from getting wet, and in a secured area that limits access, especially for children and animals. Lock chemicals up, separate incompatible chemicals such as acid and chlorine, and keep them cool in a well-ventilated area. Never mix chlorine products with acid, which could create toxic gases. If a label says to pre-dissolve, add the chemical to water, never water to the chemical. Do not store pool chemicals near a Hayward heater.[7][8][9][10]

Read the product label before every use, and dose only as the label directs for your pool's volume.[9]

Before a hurricane or severe storm

These steps come from UF/IFAS Extension in Florida. Follow local emergency officials first.

  1. Keep the water in. Do not drain or partially drain the pool before a storm. Water pressing on a drained pool could crack it, and a pool drained below the skimmer could burn up the pump motor. Keep it full until the storm passes and the ground drains.[2]
  2. Keep furniture out of the pool. Never put outdoor furniture in the pool: water and chemicals corrode it and can permanently stain the pool finish.[2]
  3. Shock only by the label. You may shock the pool before the storm arrives. Follow the product label exactly.[2][9]
  4. Know your pump guidance. Under this Florida advice, keep the pump running while the power is still on. Bestway filter pumps are not recommended for use during a storm when thunder or lightning is present. Follow your own manual. Do not go out to tend the pool during the storm: there is no safe place outside when thunderstorms are in the area.[2][6][11]
  5. Bestway above-ground pools: do not overfill, as this could make the pool collapse. After heavy rainfall you may need to empty some water to get the level right. Follow your own pool's manual.[12]

SafetyThere is no safe place outside when thunderstorms are in the area. Immediately get out and away from ponds, lakes and other bodies of water. When you hear thunder, move to a substantial building with electricity or plumbing, or an enclosed, metal-topped vehicle with windows up. Stay there at least 30 minutes after the last thunder.[3][11]

After the storm

  1. Check for hazards first. Do not start any cleanup until the area is safe, with no downed power lines. Watch for unexpected wildlife.[2]
  2. Remove debris before it breaks down and stains the pool finish.[2]
  3. Lower the level only when the ground has dried. Once standing water around the pool has cleared, bring the water level back down. Never turn your back on a draining pool.[2]
  4. Check your chemical levels again once cleanup is finished.[2]

When to call a professionalCall a qualified technician before using a heater that has been under water. On Hayward heaters, do not use the heater if any part has been under water, and installation and service must be done by authorized, qualified personnel. If you smell gas, follow these steps from Hayward's heater manual: do not try to light any appliance; do not touch any electrical switch or use any phone in the building; call your gas supplier right away from a neighbor's phone and follow its instructions; and if you can't reach the supplier, call the fire department.[10]

Winter swimming in a mild climate

In moderate climates, Hayward H150FD–H500FD heaters can keep running through short cold spells. Do not use them for freeze protection or to hold the water just above freezing. Check your own heater manual.[10]

  • Pool covers both reduce evaporation and help the water hold heat.[13]
  • If you will be away for several days, turn the heater off or the temperature down.[4]

SafetySafety covers help keep children out of the pool during unsupervised periods. Solar (bubble) covers are not safety covers. A cover is one layer, not a replacement for the others: use a four-sided fence children cannot climb, and designate an adult Water Watcher.[4][14]

When to call a professional

When to call a professionalGet help for electrical wiring, any gas heater service, and any heater that has been under water. On Hayward H150FD–H500FD heaters, licensed electricians must do all electrical wiring, and a qualified installer, service agency or the gas supplier must do any service. If you cannot find a leak yourself, consider a professional plumber or a pool leak detection specialist. For a chemical exposure, call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222.[10][15][16]

Common questions

Should I drain my pool before a hurricane?
No. Florida extension advice is to keep the pool full until after the storm. A partially drained pool could crack under the weight of water outside it.[2]
Why does my chlorine drop so fast in summer?
Sunlight, sweat and dirt from swimmers all reduce chlorine. Test chlorine and pH at least twice a day.[1]
Can I put patio furniture in the pool so it doesn't blow away?
Never. Water and chemicals corrode furniture and can permanently stain the pool finish.[2]
Can my heater protect the pool from a freeze?
Not if it is a Hayward H150FD–H500FD heater: do not use those heaters for freeze protection. Check your own heater's manual.[10]
