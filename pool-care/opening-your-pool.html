How to Open Your Pool for the Season

Open your home pool safely: clear water off the cover, reconnect equipment per your manuals, test to CDC targets, and re-check fences, alarms and drains.

  • Guides
  • Pool stores
  • Service companies

Applies to: In-ground and above-ground home pools reopening after winter; pumps, filters, heaters, heat pumps, covers and safety barriers

Quick answer

Pump water off the cover before you take it off, and keep children away while you work. Have your equipment prepared and reconnected according to your manuals, and have a pool professional remove the winterizing plugs. Then test and balance the water to CDC's targets, and re-check fences, gates, alarms and drain covers before anyone swims.[1][2][3][4]

Pool upkeep is not drowning prevention.Keep the fence, self-latching gate, alarms and close adult supervision in place, and keep children away from chemicals and equipment while you work. See backyard pool safety layers.

Start with safety: the cover and the kids

SafetyA cover with water on it is a drowning hazard. A young child can drown in just inches of water. Never leave a child unsupervised near a pool, so keep children inside or with another adult while you clear and remove the cover. Keep the control for a powered cover out of their reach.[2][5]

Opening the pool also wakes up the chemical shelf. Lock chemicals up to protect people and animals. Guidance for pool operators goes further: secure chemical storage to limit access, especially for children and animals.[6][7]

Remove the cover

  1. Use a cover pump to take the water off the top of the cover.[1]
  2. Take the cover off slowly so you don't drop debris into the pool.[1]
  3. Lay the cover somewhere it can air-dry before you store it.[1]
  4. If you're unsure how to remove your cover, call your pool professional.[1]

Reconnect and inspect the equipment

Have a pool professional prepare the equipment before anything runs, including removing winterizing plugs and replacing the pump's drain plugs. Ask a professional to check for leaks and cracks too, especially after a harsh winter.[1]

SafetyFrom the Hayward S200 sand filter manual: failing to remove plugs used in winterization from the pool or spa suction outlets can increase the potential for suction entrapment.[8]

  • Hayward Max-Flo pumps: all suction and discharge valves and the filter air relief valve must be open when starting the pump. If water leaks from the pump or filter, do not start the pump. If there is no leak, stand at least 10 feet from the pump and filter when starting, then go back and close the filter's air relief valve once a steady stream of water comes out. Skipping these steps could cause severe personal injury. Follow your own pump and filter manuals.[9]
  • Never run a Max-Flo pump without water.[9]
  • For Hayward variable-speed heat pumps (HP50CLEE, HP65CLEE, HP75CLEE), fill the system with water fully before first use after a long idle period.[10]
  • Your filter runs under pressure, so its gauge shouldn't read too high or too low. If readings look abnormal, have your pro check.[1]
  • Once the pump, filter, heater and salt or chlorine system are running, check that everything works as it should.[1]

SafetyTest the GFCI that protects your equipment as that equipment's manual directs. The Hayward S200 sand filter manual gives this procedure for the GFCI protecting the electrical equipment's supply circuit: push the test button, which should cut power, then push reset, which should restore it. If it doesn't work this way, the GFCI is defective. If it cuts power without the test button being pushed, a ground current is flowing and there may be a shock hazard: do not use the equipment. In either case, disconnect it and have a qualified service representative correct the problem before using it.[8]

Refill and add chemicals carefully

Refill to the level your own pool and equipment manuals specify. Fiberglass and in-ground vinyl liner pools should never be empty.[11]

With a Hayward Max-Flo pump, do not add chemicals directly in front of the pump suction. Follow each product's label for your pool's volume.[9][12]

Test and balance the water

Home pool owners must routinely test and adjust both chlorine and pH. Aim for a pH of 7.0 to 7.8 and at least 1 ppm chlorine in pools, or at least 2 ppm if you use cyanuric acid or stabilized chlorine.[3]

Test chlorine and pH at least twice a day, and more often when many people are swimming. Follow your test kit maker's directions.[3]

SafetyChemical safety while you work: read the product label before each use and wear safety goggles and gloves. Never mix chlorine products with acid, because toxic gases can form, and don't mix different chemicals, such as different types of chlorine products. If a label says to pre-dissolve, add the chemical to water, never water to the chemical. Lock chemicals up to protect people and animals. Guidance for pool operators adds: limit access to chemical storage, especially for children. Need help now? Call the Poison Help Line at 1-800-222-1222.[6][7][12][13]

Re-check every safety layer before the first swim

  • Drain covers: never enter a pool that has a loose, broken or missing drain cover. Hair, limbs, jewelry or swimsuits can get stuck in a drain or suction opening.[4]
  • Gates: the water should only be reachable through a self-closing, self-latching gate. A gate that fails to close and latch completely is the weak link in any fence.[4][5]
  • Alarms and covers: install a door alarm from the house to the pool area and keep it working.[4]
  • Above-ground ladders: steps or ladders can be designed to be secured, locked or removed. On a Bestway pool, remove or secure the ladder when the pool isn't in use.[5][14]
  • Opening day is a good time to review your pool safety plan.[1]

When to call a professional

When to call a professionalCall a pro for leaks or cracks you can't explain, abnormal filter pressure readings, and a GFCI that fails its test. A winterized Jandy JXi gas heater should be reassembled by a qualified professional technician in the spring. Do not use a JXi heater if any part has been under water; call a qualified service technician right away to inspect it.[1][8][15]

When to call a professionalImproper installation or operation of a Jandy JXi heater can create carbon monoxide and flue gases that can cause serious injury. On Hayward variable-speed heat pumps (HP50CLEE, HP65CLEE, HP75CLEE), only a certified technician should service the refrigerant system.[10][15]

Common questions

Can I open my pool myself?
You can do much of the cleanup, but have a pool professional prepare the equipment, including removing winterizing plugs and replacing the pump's drain plugs. A Hayward Max-Flo pump should be installed and serviced only by a qualified professional.[1][9]
What chlorine and pH should I aim for?
Aim for a pH of 7.0 to 7.8 and at least 1 ppm chlorine in pools, or at least 2 ppm with cyanuric acid. Use the product label for the amounts to add.[3][12]
Why get the water off the cover first?
Pump water off the cover before removing it. A young child can drown in just inches of water.[1][2]
